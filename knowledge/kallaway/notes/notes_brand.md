## 1. Core principles

1. **Pick the game: category famous, not mainstream famous.** Two games: mass awareness and reach (Hormozi, Cody Sanchez) or targeted awareness and efficient conversion (category famous). The second is faster to win from scratch and "you'll probably make more money per follower". It is easy to start narrow and widen later, and very hard to start broad and narrow down. Example: Kallaway puts his own channel in the category game. [S-7VkwSU0gs] IN SKILL (s1 "pick the game"). + gives the trade-offs and the one-way door.

2. **Authority density beats follower count.** Micro fame = "authority density per follower". 2M generic sports followers vs 50K collectibles followers who all see that person as the expert: the 50K account "in almost every scenario" earns more. Every follower who does not trust you is clear water in red food colouring (trust dilution), which he calls "the number one killer for why personal brands stop being able to monetize". So buying followers or running follower ads is a mistake. Test: if you polled all your followers on "who is the number one person you trust in this niche", would they say you? If not, "you have a density problem", not a follower problem. [1ilMGCxJBQY] NEW.

3. **Viewer drift kills conversion.** Without a clear target viewer, content drifts broader to chase views, the messaging turns generic, and "you have less and less conversion potency in your audience as it grows". Fix: the avatar filter. Before every video ask "will this provide value for my desired ideal viewer?" If not, it's an immediate no. [S-7VkwSU0gs] IN SKILL (s1 filter). + names the failure ("viewer drift").

4. **Target authority statement: "I help X person with Y thing in Z way."** His own: "I help business owners scale their businesses faster with social media or YouTube." [S-7VkwSU0gs; 46t_cn0lx2E has a worksheet] IN SKILL (s1, worded "to get Z"). + his exact wording is "in Z way".

5. **Filter by the offer's outcome.** Newer version of the filter: (1) what product or offer am I selling? (2) what outcome does my buyer want most? (3) for each video, will watching it get them one step closer to that outcome? Yes, make it; no, don't. "Own the minimum sized category that would align with the offer you're selling." [ceRZVxO8KF8] NEW (sharper than the s1 filter because it ties topic to the offer).

6. **Narrow and single wins association.** Rule 1: the narrower the category, the faster you build association in the same time (the spiderweb: "video storytelling for business owners" vs "business"). Rule 2: owning one category is easier than owning five, because each extra topic splits your content minutes. Start narrow, then widen (Cody Sanchez went from boring businesses to general business; Steven Bartlett from CEO talks to general culture). [wKdElDeMXR0] IN SKILL (s8). + the two rules and the reason (minutes split).

7. **Niche bullseye.** Draw rings, for example: social growth for business owners > social growth for anyone > content on any platform > marketing > business. Inside the bullseye you can (1) pick topics that are valuable to the avatar every time, (2) use examples and pain points they have "100% of the time", (3) use the words they think in, and (4) "most importantly", have proof that matches your claims for those people. Widen one ring only as authority and proof grow. [S-7VkwSU0gs] NEW (tool).

8. **Contrarian beliefs are the core of positioning.** "If your opinions are the same as everyone else, you are just a derivative that is smaller than the main person. You'll always lose." Most brands have 3 to 5 macro contrarian frames with smaller takes under them. Build a "contrarian take vault": when you violently disagree with someone in your space, write down your alternative. Content recipe: pick a subtopic, state the common belief, introduce your contrarian frame, explain why it's right. "This is all I do in every single video." Guardrail: this is not edgy for its own sake. It is "something that you believe that you think most people are getting wrong". [S-7VkwSU0gs, wKdElDeMXR0, 6G1wIdxWF5w] IN SKILL (s8 running list, s3 hook spine). + 3 to 5 macro frames, the vault trigger, and the 4-step video recipe.

9. **Data picks the topic, you supply the take.** Every piece of content = topic + take. Weak brands guess the topic and copy the take; premium brands use data for the topic and creativity for the take. "Your gut is typically wrong when it comes to topic selection." Data "is just telling you which ponds to fish in". [46t_cn0lx2E, 6G1wIdxWF5w, ImzoNTrgvFg] IN SKILL (s2 six pipes). + the clean split: research the topic, never outsource the take.

10. **Signal = the share of your minutes that are valuable.** Out of 100 minutes someone spent with you, how many were valuable? If you cover four topics, your best possible signal on any one of them is your share of minutes. Keep it high with content that is "non-obvious and tactically implementable". [wKdElDeMXR0, cuVyTmbOZjk] IN SKILL (s1 trust formula, partly). + the ratio framing.

11. **Every video is a standalone marketing agent ("flashbang brain").** Viewers keep only micro memory (the last 30 s) and long-term face recognition, with "zero memory" in between. So assume every viewer has never seen you, and push every video to a lead-magnet CTA for an email. "Before... out of every 10, maybe one or two I would try to drive them to a lead magnet. But I don't see it that way anymore." Nine times out of 10 (or 99 out of 100) this is not a sales ask. It means trading more help for an email, to get the viewer "from the chaos of the feed into the relative calmness of the email inbox". He also says you can then measure each video on its own. His result: about 3K to 33K followers in about 50 days and "tens of thousands of emails". [ceRZVxO8KF8] IN SKILL (s7 one keyword CTA on every reel). + the reason, and that he changed his mind (see s5).

12. **Offer before content.** "Business is just traffic, funnels, and offers." Build a validated offer with proof first, then content: "You can figure out how to make content work for almost any offer, but you can't always make any offer work for any audience." [bqzd0h0gmU0] NEW (the skill only says the funnel must be live before posting).

13. **Trust is a bank; more pain needs less trust.** Each trust signal deposits coins. A viewer's pain is "inversely correlated to the amount of trust needed to get them to go down the funnel". More trust lets you raise the price without raising the felt risk. [pSIY9ngEZC8] NEW.

14. **Influence by suggestion, not by claims.** Direct selling ("I'm the best, your system is broken, buy mine") trips the viewer's guard. Showing ("the five strategies I used to grow these businesses to over 20 million a year") lets them reach the same conclusion themselves, and "when people come to conclusions on their own, they trust you much faster." [cuVyTmbOZjk] NEW. The skill's "observation not attack" (s5) is related but is about tone.

15. **Proof early: the trust anchor.** On short form, "in the second or third line after the first line in your hook, you reference some proof." That can be results, an experiment, a case study, or hours spent. Check: "Why should the viewer trust this to be true?" [6G1wIdxWF5w] IN SKILL (s3 lock-in zone, proof right after hook). + the exact line position and the self-check question.

16. **Go deep on one home platform ("violent volume").** Pick one home platform and post heavily; spreading across 5 platforms means "five algorithms... with a fifth of your time". For most people that is 5 to 7 shorts or 1 to 2 long-form videos a week. Don't expand until you've dominated. Regular business owners pick one content type and one platform and stay on it for 6 months. Choose by where your customers are densest ("fish where the fish are"). [46t_cn0lx2E, bqzd0h0gmU0] NEW (the skill has volume only for Sam's own channel).

17. **Instagram is the home platform for most.** "The highest value [platform] to build on by far is Instagram", because the algorithm is mature and the ManyChat comment-to-click ecosystem works. LinkedIn is second (valuable users, but newer video and weaker links). TikTok has almost no value unless you sell a consumer commodity. YouTube is "king by far for lead gen and sales" but hard to start on: do shorts for 3 to 6 months first. [ceRZVxO8KF8] NEW. Note bqzd0h0gmU0 says B2B services should be on LinkedIn or YouTube (see s5).

18. **Quantity without lowering the bar.** "When supply is constrained, speed wins. But when supply is infinite, quality wins." Before posting: "Is that the best I could have done given my time and skills today?" and "If this was the only thing people could see from me, would I be proud to put my name on it?" Be the "gold standard" in your micro niche: interesting and valuable, novel, very clear, packaged with as much polish as you can. [46t_cn0lx2E, ceRZVxO8KF8] NEW.

19. **Obsession is the input.** Doing leads to results, results to proof, proof to trust, trust to association. "If you just talk about a thing that you actually don't do... fake obsession... people can sniff that out." Gut check: "Are you genuinely excited to make the next 100 pieces of content about this topic?" [wKdElDeMXR0, 46t_cn0lx2E] NEW.

20. **Expertise vs personality mix.** Personal brand = expertise (beliefs plus signal) + personality (messaging plus taste). "Consumer niches almost always prioritize personality over expertise, but B2B is the reverse." [wKdElDeMXR0] Partly IN SKILL (s5: expertise niches favour tactical content). + the B2B rule.

21. **Association at scale.** A personal brand is "just association at scale": say and show the same few things until they come to mind on reflex. Exercise: "if I could buy one or two or three words or terms in someone's memory, what terms would I buy?" Objects help (his hat, black hoodie, set, round sunglasses; Hormozi's hat and tank). "People have a weird way of remembering objects instead of faces and names." [S-7VkwSU0gs, wKdElDeMXR0] NEW.

22. **Amplifiers.** Clips of you posted by others (clip accounts, podcast reactions) spread the association beyond your own posting time. [wKdElDeMXR0] NEW.

23. **Simple formats are winning for busy owners.** Business owners are moving from heavy-edit formats to "the middle" between raw rambling and cinematic. That middle is storytelling formats (scenarios, ranking, tier list, three levels) that "can be one-shotted and edited fully on an iPhone". [bqzd0h0gmU0] NEW. This matters for Sam's pitch (see s4 and s5).

24. **Patience in years, not weeks.** "1 year to get average, 3 years to get good, and 5 years to get world-class." "Most of the content game is just tricking yourself into not quitting." Don't anchor on outliers like Kumar (1M followers in 5 days). "The only thing that makes you lose is when you stop posting." [bqzd0h0gmU0, ceRZVxO8KF8] IN SKILL (s1 "you cannot lose if"). + the expectations framing.

25. **The stealing economy.** Copying is "a creativity tax". To defend, make content that is harder to copy: (1) real-life sets, (2) longer form, (3) ideas from your own experience. Studying and remixing principles and formats is fine; word-for-word copying makes you "a derivative of a derivative". [ceRZVxO8KF8] NEW.

26. **Transpositioning.** Don't clone the leader in your niche. You won't outrun someone ahead of you with a compounding lead. Take formats and structures that work in distant niches and bring them into yours. "That's really where the alpha and sauce is." [1ilMGCxJBQY, ceRZVxO8KF8] Partly IN SKILL (s2 "other media translated", s8 samvex "copy mechanics"). + aimed at niches, not media.

27. **Copied visual styles credit the original.** "When you copy a visual style that most people think of one person for, most of the value accrues back to me." Substance and delivery are hard to copy, so they're a moat. [1ilMGCxJBQY] NEW.

28. **Storytelling era.** Trend-chasing and overstimulating edits worked when supply was short. Now the winners are "premium storytelling": characters, pacing, narrative arcs, world-building, tension. The format and production level can vary. Alex Garcia (premium consumer brands) says fast cuts and lots of on-screen text "not really working anymore" for those brands. [ImzoNTrgvFg] Partly IN SKILL (s5 story). + the five pillars and the "premium brands" warning.

29. **Connective tissue: guide the binge.** On short form: numbered series, the link-video button, story highlights as a curated binge playlist. Don't say "watch this next" out loud on shorts (do say it on YouTube). [6G1wIdxWF5w] NEW.

30. **Binge bank.** If a hooked viewer taps your profile and finds 6 videos, the binge stops. You need a back catalogue, so push quantity at your minimum quality. [6G1wIdxWF5w] NEW.

## 2. Frameworks (step by step, his wording where he gives formulas)

### 2.1 Attention conversion funnel: "The ONLY 4 Things You Need to Make Sales from EVERY Video" [pSIY9ngEZC8] NEW (s7 of the skill covers ramp and offer only)
The four steps multiply: "if even one of them is a zero, the whole equation is zero."

**Step 1. Bull's-eye signaling.** The viewer must think "that's the exact thing I'm struggling with" (pain) or "that's the exact thing I want" (perceived solution). The brain sorts a video into one of three buckets: potential solution, entertainment, or irrelevant. Only "potential solution" leads to a buyer; entertainment views are "junk food". Five tactics:
1. Pick specific topics and examples tied to the ideal viewer's pain points that also line up with your core offer. His offer is content systems, so his topics are what to make, iteration, strategy, storytelling, monetization and systems.
2. An extremely clear first 1 to 2 sentences that signal the pain. His own: "Today we're talking about monetizing your content. If you want to make videos that actually drive leads and sales, there are four things you have to have in your content workflow. I call this the attention conversion funnel." That is line 1 topic, line 2 pain, line 3 teased solution. "The biggest reason people can't drive sales is confusion."
3. Name the emotion of not having the result (frustration, sadness, anger, confusion).
4. Show or describe the before and after.
5. Power words, names, term branding, so the solution feels new.

**Step 2. Trust bank and trust ladder.** The viewer's next question is "can I really trust this person?" It is a binary gate. The ladder, weakest to strongest (bottom rung about 1 coin, top about 40 to 50):
- 8: Third-party verification (a study, stat or poll)
- 7: Cold endorsement or testimonial ("I helped Mark, a business owner in San Antonio"). Weak because it's unverifiable and from an unknown person.
- 6: Borrowed verified proof ("I used Hormozi's value framework")
- 5: Proof of personal results
- 4: Group or warm testimonials: rapid-fire several names and their results. Much stronger if one name is recognisable. A pattern beats a one-off.
- 3: Demonstration of ability in the content itself. Testimonials can be faked; visible competence can't.
- 2: Proof of audience (a large audience that clearly wasn't botted)
- 1: Warm endorsement from someone the viewer already knows and respects (shared in a Slack, sent by DM). This is why referral programmes and share loops matter.
Rungs stack within one video. His usual stack is rungs 2, 3 and 5 every time, sometimes 1, 4, 6 or 7. Short form has less room for rungs. More pain needs fewer coins.

**Step 3. Clear ramp (the off-ramp from the content highway).** Three types:
1. Lead magnet: his favourite and the lowest friction. Captures email.
2. Sales page: for low-ticket offers, or when trust is high enough that a warm viewer buys high-ticket without a call.
3. Sales call or DM conversation: for high-ticket offers where the buyer needs hand-holding and trust is lower.
Rule: "make sure you have at least one for every piece of content and try your best to align it as close as possible to whatever problem or solution you signaled." His example: talk about New York City for 8 hours, then offer an exit to Tulsa, Oklahoma, and nobody takes it. "The simplest ramp I recommend everybody getting into place right away... a free lead magnet that collects emails in exchange for some value... ideally tightly [connected]." (The skill's s13 #3 already has video topic = freebie topic.)

**Step 4. Aligned offer.** The buyer asks: "If I hand over this money, how likely am I to get the result they're promising?" There are two levers: price (risk up) and trust (risk down). The **flip point** is the price where felt risk outweighs felt reward. His 1-hour call example: $1, $100 and $1,000 are no-brainers for his ICP; at $10,000 most flinch; at $100,000 almost everyone is out. His ICP flips somewhere between $1K and $10K. Two options: price below the flip point (low ticket, volume), or just above it with a guarantee or other de-risk mechanism. Trust coins let you push the flip point higher. His **10x rule**: "in the first 30 days after someone pays, if they do what I say... they will get 10x or more the return on that money." The offer's messaging must match the content ("content offer fit"). Diagnosis: if content isn't turning views into dollars, one of the four is zero, or "you haven't waited long enough".

### 2.2 90-day personal brand plan [S-7VkwSU0gs, newest] NEW
**Days 1 to 3 (2 to 3 hours up to 2 to 3 days): the Personal Brand Positioning Canvas.** Three questions:
1. Objective: which game? Mass awareness and reach, or targeted awareness and efficient conversion (category famous).
2. Target audience: the ideal viewer, the target authority statement ("I help X person with Y thing in Z way"), and the avatar filter.
3. The four personal brand pillars:
   a. **Niche targeting**: the bullseye rings (see principle 7).
   b. **Contrarian beliefs**: a vault; 3 to 5 macro frames with smaller takes under them.
   c. **Association and common objects**: which 1 to 3 terms you'd "buy" in memory; recurring objects, set and outfit. This one doesn't need to be final yet.
   d. **Points of uniqueness**: map 5 to 10 core competitors on 5 dimensions: topic, substance, avatar, format, visuals. Find the white space and "plant a stake". ("Previously I've... talked about seven points of uniqueness. I think it just bubbles up into these five.")
"Winning in personal branding is not about adding stuff. It's about subtracting": guardrails on what you talk about, to whom, how, and where.

**Days 4 to about 60: Phase 1, testing and experimentation.** "A content formula equals a topic plus a format plus a visual layout." Target: 3 to 4 core topics, 1 to 3 formats and 1 to 3 visual layouts, giving roughly 5 to 10 formulas. Cadence: "three the first week, five the second week, and then seven per week for the next six weeks. That's about 50 videos." Test "widely and wildly", not caring about performance. The only filter: "Is the video going to provide value for the [ideal viewer]?" Judge each formula on four questions: (1) does it perform on the metric we care about (views, followers or conversion)? (2) does it attract the ideal viewer? Judge qualitatively from comments, DMs, and the bios of new followers. (3) do we enjoy making it, so we don't burn out? (4) does it look like something we're proud of? Topic research: competitors' top 50 to 100 videos analysed with an AI tool.

**About day 60 to 90 and on to 6 months: Phase 2, strategy alignment and system scaling.** Double down on the formulas that work, cut the rest, repeat. After 6 months (the first sprint) you have a set roster. Formulas burn out as people copy them, so go back to testing: "that oscillation is the art of content over time."

### 2.3 The 3-2-1 content strategy [1ilMGCxJBQY] NEW
"Three different topic buckets that you make two videos each per week... The seventh video is... the chaos agent."
- Bucket A (broad): still builds authority in the niche, but wider, "so you can have a chance at virality".
- Buckets B and C (narrow): "very valuable to your specific ideal viewer avatar and buyer". Sources: known pain points, wanted outcomes, client conversations. "Not meant to go super viral... meant to go deeper and drive more conversion and trust accrual per view."
- Video 7, the chaos agent: a triple down on a bucket, or a random test from another niche ("controlled chaos").
Choosing buckets: guess, or (his recommendation) research 20 to 25 competitors with 25K to 250K followers, outliers from the last 3 months, engagement at least 2% (this screens out paid brand deals), 50 to 100 videos analysed. Prompt (his wording, trimmed): "My goal is to build a high-value authority audience in [niche]. Build a visual distribution that breaks down the top-performing topic buckets... Screen out any video that doesn't make sense for my niche... come up with one broader-reaching viral topic bucket and two more narrow topic buckets for the avatar." Review every 2 weeks: if a bucket's 4 videos all flop, kill it and promote a new one (from research or from what the chaos agent found).

### 2.4 Unique positioning angle (UPA), 7 attributes [1ilMGCxJBQY] NEW (S-7 later compresses this to 5)
Stack as many as you can; the more crowded the niche, the more you need.
1. Topic selection: topics nobody covers.
2. Substance depth: common topics, deeper value.
3. Unique stories or scenarios: experience nobody else can claim without lying.
4. Avatar specificity: an underserved slice (his example: social media for mortgage brokers).
5. Delivery style: tone, cadence, dress.
6. Storytelling format: e.g. challenge videos where everyone else does tier lists.
7. Visual format: e.g. cinematic where everyone shoots raw.
His own stack: substance plus delivery plus visuals plus one open topic (AI workflows) = "one of one".

### 2.7 Personal brand formula [wKdElDeMXR0] NEW (extends the skill's s1 trust formula)
"Personal brand value equals viewers times minutes times stickiness." Association is the output: association = world building (spreads to viewers) x obsession (makes the minutes) x positioning (makes them stick).
- Minutes: make more of them, and keep more on target (narrow topics).
- "Callaway's law": the longer the content, the stickier each minute (short < long < live).
- Stickiness has 5 drivers: longer formats, multiple surfaces, more contrarian beliefs, higher signal, clearer messaging.
- Positioning has 4 parts: beliefs (contrarian), messaging ("dealer's choice"; only clarity and consistency are required), signal, taste ("the consistency of your choices over time aligned with what the viewer thinks is quality").
- World building has 6 parts: surfaces, objects, characters, citizens, aesthetics, interoperability (the "rails" from short form to YouTube to email to community).
- **Modern personal brand stack:** "One short form platform for reach, one long form platform for depth, and one nurture platform, ideally email or private community." Cadence: "seven short form videos a week, one to two long forms a week." "This is going to take hundreds and hundreds of posts."

### 2.8 Fandom funnel and fan attribution pathway [6G1wIdxWF5w] NEW
Stages by content minutes consumed: unaware > first-time viewer (1 min) > casual watcher (2 to 5) > follower (5 to 10, "the most important line") > casual fan (20 to 30; you see the same names in the comments) > active supporter (30 to 60, about "40 to 50 short-form videos or three to six full long-form") > super fan (hours: hundreds of shorts, dozens of long-form videos). "For every 1,000 people that start... maybe only five to 10" become fans at the bottom. Pac-Man: each video is one dot.
**Fan attribution pathway (short form):** a video hooks them > they tap the profile > in about half a second they judge follower count (over 50K: most follow on reflex; 10K to 50K: most hesitate and want more videos; under 10K: "people typically pass") > they watch the pinned videos (usually the most-viewed) > video 3 or 4 > follow or leave. A non-follower sees you again in 3 to 7 days and the audition restarts with minutes banked. YouTube: about 30 s of leash, then 3 to 5 min, then the profile; around 25K subs is the reflex threshold.
**Four forces:** bull's-eye relatability, parasocial transformation ("3-2-1 transformation": 3 things that change how they think, 2 that change how they act, 1 they applied and got a real result; then "it'll feel like they owe you"), deep one-of-one expertise ("less influencer, more sherpa"), vibe magnet.
**Seven shifts:**
1. Audience of one ("is this valuable for the one person...")
2. Data-driven topic selection (top 30 to 50 videos into a topic distribution, every 2 to 4 weeks)
3. Non-obvious insights ("probably the most important")
4. Binge bank
5. Connective tissue
6. Trust anchor in line 2 or 3
7. Authentic delivery = genuinely interested + energy + visual packaging you're proud of

### 2.9 Power of suggestion: three levels of obsession [cuVyTmbOZjk] NEW
- **Level 1, signal layer:** relevant to the ideal viewer + non-obvious + validated. Nothing is planted; "you never once said I'm the best".
- **Level 2, belief layer:** a contrasting frame (then vs now, A vs B, the viewer's current state vs the after) + a relatable character who started where the viewer is + small cost, large reward. Example: "0 to 100K on YouTube in 5 months" changed viewers' belief about how fast growth can be, without arguing it.
- **Level 3, action layer:** quantitative proof of the result ("from two to 15,000 followers in 6 weeks", not "this will help you grow") + steps "broken down... to the atomic unit". Don't command: "it feels like homework". Show what you did so they connect input to result.
- **Five-item checklist for every video:** (1) relevant and relatable to the ideal viewer, (2) small effort, big reward, (3) non-obvious frame, (4) validated with proof ("first get the proof, then make the content based on the proof"), (5) tactically implementable steps. Mistake to avoid: making the solution complex because "complex equals valuable". Simplicity drives action.

### 2.10 Regular business owner playbook and content machine [bqzd0h0gmU0] NEW (batch logic partly IN SKILL s9)
"One offer, one content type, one content platform for one customer avatar."
1. Validate the offer first: talk to people in the industry; find what they'd give up (money or time); choose a delivery model (tell them, do it for them, give a tool); price high, medium or low, one-time or recurring; deliver for real customers until you have proof. This takes "maybe a couple weeks if you hustle".
2. One content type on one platform for 6 months, where customer density is highest.
3. Simple storytelling formats.
4. Content machine: learn to make it at 7/10 > cut time per rep at 7/10 (systems, AI, humans in the loop) > push to 9/10 > rebuild the system at 9/10 > raise volume at 9/10.
5. Short-form system: batches of 10. Batch 1 = 10 outliers (5x+) from a 20 to 30 channel watch list, keeping topic, format, hook, structure and edit, changing only the substance (your non-obvious POV). If one of yours is a 5x outlier, 3 of the next 10 replicate it. Repeat until you have "three viral winners, three recipes"; then you have a machine "for consistently driving leads".

## 3. Content plan a business coach could run (only from what he says)

- **Before posting:** a validated offer with proof [bqzd]; the positioning canvas (game = category fame; target authority statement "I help [coaches / agency owners / ...] with [Y] in [Z way]"; bullseye; 3 to 5 contrarian frames; 1 to 3 terms to own; a 5-dimension competitor map) [S-7]; one lead magnet per main topic, live [pSIY, ceRZ].
- **Platform:** Instagram as the home platform (most people) [ceRZ], or wherever their buyers are densest; for B2B services he once said LinkedIn or YouTube [bqzd]. One platform only until dominated [46t]. Add YouTube after 3 to 6 months of shorts if they want depth and lead gen [ceRZ, wKd].
- **Mix (3-2-1 per week) [1ilMG]:**
  - 2x broad bucket: a coaching-adjacent topic with reach, still authority-building.
  - 2x narrow bucket B and 2x narrow bucket C: built from client sales-call pain points and the outcomes buyers want, tied to the offer.
  - 1x chaos agent: a triple down or a format from another niche (transpositioning).
  - Kill a bucket after 2 weeks if all 4 of its videos flop.
- **Ramp-up cadence [S-7]:** 3 videos in week 1, 5 in week 2, then 7 a week for 6 weeks (about 50 videos in 60 days); then double down and cut. Steady state 5 to 7 shorts a week [46t, wKd].

## 1. Core principles

Format: rule / why (his words, condensed) / his example / source.

### Story mechanics
1. **Between beats the connector is "but" or "therefore", never "and then".** IN SKILL (adds: he counts them, 4 "but" loops in the first 30 s of his best Stanley Cup video). Why: conflict opens loops, context closes them; "and then" piles detail and people leave. Example: Stanley Cup script ("but the real question is how'd they do it", "but it gets even better"). t5Z, WluuZB.
2. **Vary sentence length; one sentence per line, the right edge must look jagged.** IN SKILL. Why: equal-length sentences are monotonous, people churn. Example: Gary Provost passage. t5Z.
3. **Write to one close friend, like a text or voice note.** IN SKILL (adds: tape a photo of the friend under the lens; his own first 50 videos "talk at you"). Why: removes the "am I being sold to" barrier. t5Z.
4. **Write the last line first ("last dab"); in a loop it sets up the first line.** IN SKILL (adds: he writes first and last line, leaves space, fills the middle). Example: 9-hitter sets up top of the order; Nolan. t5Z.
5. **Story lens: pick the angle nobody took.** IN SKILL. Taylor Swift / NFL business impact, about a million views. t5Z.
6. **First line = punchy and indicative of the plot; show while you tell.** IN SKILL. His claim: "visual hooks are 10x more effective than audio-only hooks" (no data shown). Example: Epic Gardening strawberry on screen before a word is said. t5Z.
7. **Dopamine is the prediction chemical, not the pleasure chemical. The high is the waiting, not the answer.** IN SKILL only as the addiction loop list; the WHY is NEW. Example: vending machine (predictable, not addictive) vs slot machine (uncertain, addictive); the only difference is uncertainty. KyC8r.
8. **Stakes = a character to root for + something at risk + urgency.** IN SKILL as one line; tactics NEW. Stakes do not mean life or death, they mean "personally relevant for your ideal viewer". Urgency is the multiplier ("within a week" beats "eventually"; "the call was in 2 hours" beats "later in the day"). Example: "I had 48 hours to figure out why our paid budget wasn't working or I was going to blow through the whole thing." Test: before the story starts the viewer knows WHAT is at risk, WHO it happens to, WHY it matters NOW. KyC8r.
9. **Big question: give just enough specifics for the viewer to make a prediction.** NEW tactic. Vague teasers ("something crazy happened", "you won't believe it", "things were about to change") give nothing to predict, so the later twist has nothing to contrast against. Example good: "I get on the call with my client, and within 30 minutes she tells me something that completely changes my strategy for the next 6 months." Bad: "I had a call with a client and it went well." Tactic: before any reveal, stop, back up, add context so they can guess first. Load the biggest question early: longer drip. KyC8r.
10. **Head fake must be surprising AND instantly logical (clues planted).** IN SKILL as "fair, clues planted"; the 2-step build is NEW: (1) work out what a normal viewer predicts from what you said, (2) reveal something that contrasts it but they accept as true right away. Cheap surprise = random = confusion. Example: dragon is a prisoner, not a guard (it was pulling away, scared); magician shows the empty hat first. KyC8r.
11. **Re-hook: close one loop and open the next in the same breath. No gap.** IN SKILL as one line; phrases NEW. Casino metric "time per hand": shrink the distance between hits. Example: "And that's when she told me she was doubling her budget, which would have been amazing if I hadn't just signed an exclusivity agreement with her competitor the night before." vs dead air: "She doubled her budget and that was a great call. Anyways..." Danger zones = every point where a section ends. Relay race: the baton passes while both runners move. KyC8r.
12. **Contrast is the engine under everything.** IN SKILL for hooks; extending it to every "haymaker" sentence is NEW. Curiosity = gap between what they expect and what you say. Split a clean point in half: lean them to A, flip to B. Don't force it on every sentence (sounds "friction heavy"). pcnrzBwoVUk.
13. **Expectations vs reality is the whole game.** NEW framing. Reality beats expectation = they keep watching; worse = they leave. A summary of known stuff can never beat expectation. 7I50.
14. **Interesting = shock value = distance between what they already know and what you tell them.** NEW ("law of interesting"). Example: Russian engineer claims a personal hydrogen reactor in his garage, he anchored the video on it, "40 million views in 5 days". _Z11.
15. **Addictive = relevant + novel + interesting.** NEW. Relevance is easy (call the viewer out); novelty and interest are the hard part in "boring" niches. LvuoNlYRs7g.
16. **People buy when they understand exactly what you said: clarity = trust = conversion.** NEW ("clarity purification", he rewrites until 100% clear). W--tE.
17. **Two "aha" moments early earn the follow, and that's where the CTA goes.** NEW. His claim: one aha earns a save or like; two prove you are a "goldmine" and get the follow ("10x trust", no data). Put the aligned CTA right after aha #2 (reciprocity peak). W--tE.
18. **The job of a conversion story is to de-risk the purchase.** IN SKILL partly (proof lever); hierarchy NEW: client proof and your own proof > demonstration of ability in the content. Info and demonstration only get you so far; high-ticket needs proof. W--tE.
19. **Hedging opens "exit doors".** IN SKILL ("flat, no hedge"); the swap list is NEW (see 2.6). pcnrzBwoVUk.
20. **Never break your own illusion; deliver like a secret, not a billboard.** NEW. "Don't show the mascot": do not follow a fresh angle with "of course, this is just circadian biology, known for decades". "Gossip whisperer" ("Okay, so not that many people know about this yet...") beats "town crier" ("Huge news! This changes everything!") because salesy tone triggers distrust. LvuoNlYRs7g.
21. **Never fake urgency or proof.** NEW. Fake deadlines kill trust and feel like selling; "if the proof isn't there you don't manufacture a lie". LvuoNlYRs7g.
22. **The old story arc (slow build to one climax) is wrong online.** NEW (7 mistakes). Why: "time compression"; his claim "80% of your viewers will bounce before the rising action". WluuZB.
23. **Viewers have an invisible timer (5, 15, 30 s). When it hits zero without "what's in it for me", they leave.** NEW. WluuZB, pcnrzBwoVUk (hourglass version).
24. **Atomic shareability: the idea must shrink to a unit a viewer can retell after one watch.** NEW. Example: "The British are coming." WluuZB.
25. **The idea is the most important part; a strong hook cannot save a weak idea.** IN SKILL. pirnGDCZD3Y.
26. **Data beats gut for hit rate; you can still experiment, the hit rate is just lower.** IN SKILL. pirnGDCZD3Y.
27. **Every product sells an A to B transformation; stories must make them want B.** NEW framing. W--tE.
28. **AI for pieces, not for the whole script.** NEW. He does not like AI writing scripts end to end ("feels very artificial"); uses research and hook variants, then edits by hand. _Z11.

### Delivery and likeability (dopamine ladder rung 5)
29. **Likeability levers: attractiveness, vibe (dress, posture), smiling (joy transfers through the lens), and the strongest one, actually solving their problem.** NEW detail (skill has the rung only). WluuZB.
30. **Stimulation (first 1 to 2 s) comes from colour, motion, brightness, contrast; it is processed "bottom-up" in about 200 ms without comprehension. If everyone copies the same look, viewers go numb, so own a palette.** IN SKILL (adds the why). WluuZB.

## 2. Frameworks, templates, checklists

### 2.1 Dopamine ladder (6 rungs). IN SKILL; added detail
1 Stimulation (visual, 1 to 2 s). 2 Captivation: a question pops in their head. Bigger and more relevant question = more pull. Ask yourself: "What is the most curiosity-inducing question I could trigger about this topic?" and "How well does it map to what my ideal viewer cares about?" Plant it by asking it outright or by implying it with details. Pro tips: compare a known thing to an unknown one; or frame a shocking or weird premise. 3 Anticipation: they guess. The most dopamine is just before the answer. Feed clues, then head-fake, or answer and pop a second question. They can only anticipate if the facts are clear; "rogue details" make them leave. 4 Validation: close the loop with a non-obvious answer (edu: the actual tip). Never leave the main loop open. 5 Affection (likes the messenger). 6 Revelation: "this person is a continued source of value". Education reaches 6 far more easily than entertainment. Goal = a "Pavlovian" response to the face or name. Way to get there: "deliver non-obvious, differentiated value over and over".
His live breakdown: Citizen's shoelace-in-car-door video (entertainment).

### 2.2 Addiction loop (4 steps). IN SKILL; tactics now complete
Stakes (character + risk + urgency) > Big question (specific enough to predict) > Head fake (predicted A, logical B) > Re-hook (new loop opens as the old one closes). Repeat.
Flat vs loop demo: prince / dragon / plague / tower / king at the top.
Re-hook phrases, word for word: "which would have been great except...", "but here's the problem with that...", "and that's when I realized...", "which is exactly why...".

### 2.3 Modern story arc + the 7 storytelling mistakes (WluuZB). NEW (this was gap 11a)
1. **Old arc is dead.** Modern arc: open at about 70/100 intensity (never at zero), first conflict or contrast spikes to about 90 very early, release to about 30, build to the next peak (about 75), release, repeat. The biggest peak can be first (later peaks ramp down) or last (peaks build up). His cadence of 2 to 5 minutes is long-form; "earn attention in 5-minute increments".
2. **Jumbled W's.** Old order: where/when > who/what > how/why ("Last month I was at a conference in Miami. I saw the CEO of Disney speak. He is worried about the future of entertainment because of regulation."). Modern hook stack: WHAT + WHY > WHO + HOW (credibility) > WHEN + WHERE (just colour). "The future of entertainment is going to look super different. The CEO of Disney is really worried about it. He was talking about it at a conference in Miami." "You don't lead with the icing. You lead with the cake." His claim: you lose 50% of viewers in the first 30 s, so the order of sentences matters.
3. **No re-buy-in.** After the first peak, set new stakes or a new pain point. Example (paid-ads startup): solve creative first, then "But the truth is, if you have great creative, it'll be underutilized unless you solve for this." After 3 to 4 loops close they feel you solved four problems. Why: algorithms push largely on average view duration.
4. **No villain.** Hero vs villain > hero vs nothing. The villain can be a thing, group or rival path (for startup founders: "corporate America"). Frame: "they do this, but we do that."
5. **Nothing to root for.** Make the viewer the protagonist; build common ground; say why the story matters for the target viewer. "When people root for you, they get optimistic."
6. **No atomic shareability.** Cut fluff, simple words, punchy sentences. For complex points, say it twice: once as to a colleague, once with a metaphor as to a child. (Sam's skill bans invented metaphors; see section 5.)
7. **Not painting the picture.** Use visuals; otherwise the viewer must imagine it and comprehension drops.

### 2.4 Conversion storytelling (W--tE). NEW
**a) Avatar framing, 4 conditions:** (1) built on a core pain or frustration of the ideal buyer that others don't have; (2) vocabulary signals a competence match (advanced buyer = advanced terms, beginner = beginner terms); (3) specific, concrete proof that validates the approach; (4) a curiosity path to "more info off the video". Short version: "pain point driven, aligned messaging, proof forward, and a path for more."
His hook example: "The fastest way to grow your business on social media is not by going viral. Instead, you want to run the 3-2-1 conversion strategy. This is what I used to go from zero to 100K on my own channel while also driving $100,000 in profit in the first 3 months... Here's how it works." It does: call-out ("grow your business"), contrarian take vs belief, named alternative, proof, mid-level business words (conversion, profit).
Failure modes: pain not specific enough; no proof or proof that does not match the claim; wrong words (doesn't sound like an insider); not enough curiosity.

**b) Broad to narrow (clarity) order:** Context > Stakes > Strategy > Tactics > Examples > Principles (summary). Bad storytellers skip context and stakes, or never come back to examples. Usable for short-form, YouTube segments, carousels, emails. Stakes act as a mini re-hook.

**c) Double aha > aligned CTA** (see principle 17).

**d) Emotional transformation response, 5-step script** (word-for-word example):
1. Aggravate the base pain + its emotion (fear, frustration, sadness, uncertainty, confusion). "As a business owner, it's incredibly hard to make content that gets both high views and high sales."
2. How you failed with the traditional method. "I spent months learning how to go viral. And even though I got 25 million views, it didn't translate into meaningful sales."
3. Tease the new mechanism, give it a new name. "But 2 weeks ago, I finally discovered a new video format that actually worked."
4. Real results, ideally exactly what they want. "After just two attempts with it, I drove $55,000 in profit off just 14,000 views. It's called the Conversion Method."
5. Offer-ramp CTA. "Comment method and I'll send you a full guide breaking down exactly how the process works..."
(These numbers are his example script, not data Sam can reuse.)

**e) Double zoom-out (study what persuades YOU):** zoom 1: "Is this making me trust this person enough to consider buying?" If yes, zoom 2: "What exactly did they say, show or do that made me trust them?" Usual answers: an example that matches my current struggle; naming my emotional state; their own pain > failed attempt > new solution. His case, Jeremy Haynes: (1) uses non-beginner terms I know, (2) repeats my exact target ("million-dollar months") over and over, (3) dissects businesses like mine, (4) does it consistently; bonus: funny.

**f) Buyer psychology:** people risk money today hoping for a multiple later; your story de-risks it via client proof, your own proof, or demonstrating ability inside the content.

### 2.5 Story locks (pcnrzBwoVUk). Mostly NEW
1. **Term branding** (labeling effect): name the concept; an unknown name creates anticipation ("mental quicksand"). Examples: Hormozi's value equation, Kevin Kelly's 1,000 True Fans, his own LEGO brick framework, content minutes, audience matching, trust ladder. Name contrarian ideas and best frameworks only, not everything. IN SKILL as "name the method"; the why is NEW.
2. **Embedded truths:** say it as already true. Swaps, word for word: "if you try this" > "when you try this"; "this might work because" > "the reason this works so well"; "you might notice this" > "once you see it, you can't unsee it"; "some creators might be making a mistake here" > "the mistake most creators make is this". Delete: if, maybe, might, could, probably. Use: when, the reason why, once you do this, this is how it works. Example: Sammy's hook "80% of visual storytelling is subconscious" stated as fact.
3. **Thought narration:** say what the viewer is thinking right now. "Now, the question you're probably thinking is this." "If I were you, the thing that would be biggest on my mind right now is this." "If there's one thing that you're worried about, it's probably this." Gives trust (you read my mind) + they must stay for the answer; "personalization at scale". Tactic: after each major point ask "What should the viewer be thinking right now?" and say it at a transition, a few times per video. IN SKILL only as "say the viewer's belief out loud before disproving it".
4. **Negative frames** (loss aversion; his figure: people are about twice as motivated to avoid pain as to seek gain). "Negative flip" examples: "Use this hook format" > "Stop making your hooks like this"; "Here's how to grow on YouTube" > "This is killing your YouTube growth"; "Try posting more consistently" > "This posting mistake is costing you followers". Softer version: "You're making content way harder than it has to be." Triple negative: "Never, ever, ever post a video on Instagram without using this feature." Not always; sometimes positive is right.
5. **Loop openers** (re-hook phrases between sections; his cadence: every 20 to 30 s in shorts, every 60 to 90 s in long-form). Shape: confirm what was just said was valuable + tease that the next is more valuable for a different reason, with a contrast word in the middle. Word for word: "Here's the thing though...", "But that's not even the most interesting part.", "And this is where it gets crazy.", "Most people stop here, but actually, here's what nobody talks about.", "Okay, so that was powerful, but the next one's even more important." Five sections = about 3 or 4 loop openers. His claim: retention graph holds flat mid-video instead of decaying.
6. **Contrast words:** but (strongest), actually ("Actually the data shows the opposite"), instead ("Instead of posting this, try this"), turns out ("It turns out the algorithm doesn't care about that at all"), except ("Every creator does this except the ones that are actually growing"), yet ("Everyone knows this yet almost nobody does it"). Contrast powers the other five.
You don't need all six in a short; layer what fits.

### 2.6 Script writing system: 4 blockers > 6 steps > 4 final questions (_Z11). NEW
Blockers: Interestingness ("what is actually interesting here?"), Hook, Storytelling ("how do I hold them after the hook?"), Engagement ("how do I make them like or share?").
**Shock score:** for every fact, out of 100 people, how many have NOT heard it? About 4 in 5 haven't = score about 80. Keep true facts with the highest score; write 5 to 10 bullets at the top of the doc.
**9 hook formats** (+ his example):
- Secret reveal ("Google just released four free AI trainings that make it way faster to learn AI") for news, releases, fresh tips.
- Case study ("Alex Hormozi just sold $100 million worth of books in 3 days using this five-step method"): X got Y via Z.
- Comparison ("What's the difference between a beginner creator versus an experienced one").
- Question ("Can you bake sourdough bread in under an hour using only household ingredients?").
- Education ("Here's the easiest way to use ChatGPT to create content").
- List ("These are the five best Premiere Pro editing templates that you can use for free").
- Contrarian ("Jaguar's rebrand is one of the worst design blunders in the history of the world"), best if it drives comment debate.
- Personal experience ("When I was in college, I used this sleep technique...").
- Problem ("If you struggle with acne, this is one obvious solution that will clear it right up"), for a pain they already know.
Pick by studying which 1 or 2 formats win in the niche's outliers; copy the outlier hook word for word, then swap words "mad lib style".
**4 hook commandments:** Alignment (visual, spoken, text say the same thing), Speed to value (zero delay, zero fluff), Clarity (one sentence = clear what's coming), Curiosity (opens a question). IN SKILL mostly; the 4-word checklist is NEW.
**Story structures** (he says 7, names 8) with outline and best hooks:
- Breakdown: hook > context shock > what happened/how it works > why it matters > outro. Hooks: secret reveal, question, comparison, contrarian.
- Newscaster: hook > full context > optional short take > outro. Hooks: secret reveal, question, comparison, contrarian, problem.
- Case study explainer: hook > framework (2 to 4 parts in sequence) > outro. Hooks: case study, question, comparison.
- Listicle: hook > 3 to 5 independent items > outro. Hooks: list, problem.
- Problem solver: hook > problem (+agitate) > solution > outro. Hooks: problem, question, contrarian.
- Tutorial: hook > dependent steps to one outcome > outro. Hooks: (not stated beyond education use).
- Educational / personal story: hook > personal experience story (start, middle, end) > outro. Hooks: personal experience, problem, contrarian.
Differences: list items stand alone, tutorial steps depend on each other; case study = analysis of what led to an outcome, tutorial = how-to steps.
Writing shortcut: "Imagine you were telling this story to a friend... how would you order those facts so they would understand it the most?" Then layer facts in order and add transitions.
**Engagement = emotional transfer.** 6 buckets: awe/inspiration, amusement/humor, excitement/joy, anger/outrage, surprise/shock/curiosity, sadness/empathy. Write the target emotion at the top of the page and use it as a filter; reread at the end and make it more visceral if you don't feel it.
**His 6-step order:** (1) research shocking bullets, (2) write the desired emotion on top, (3) pick hook format from niche outliers, write and check the 4 commandments + pair a visual, (4) pick story structure from niche outliers, write the outline headers, (5) slot facts in, add transitions, (6) reread through the emotion filter.
**Final 4 questions (text only, before visuals):** Is this story interesting? Is it as compressed as it can possibly be? Does this hook hook me on its own? What emotion do I feel when I finish reading?

### 2.7 Five-step script process + intro formula (7I50). Mostly NEW
Order: Packaging > Outline > Intro > Body > Outro (always in this order).
- **Packaging:** idea (one line = the pain you solve or the rabbit hole), title (must exist before the intro), thumbnail can stay loose. "Click confirmation": first lines confirm, ideally exceed, what the title promised (MrBeast pyramids + "unprecedented access"). IN SKILL for long-form.
- **Outline before intro:** bullet the points; if they are not unique, stop and research more ("measure twice, cut once"). For each point: what, why, how it fits.
- **Intro formula (short and long):** (1) immediate context = what it's about (opt-in + click confirmation), (2) state the common belief ("This is an art and it's one of the hardest things to learn"), (3) contrarian take against it ("But the good news is there is a right answer..."), (4) proof why to trust you + (5) the plan/ordered steps (4 and 5 in either order). IN SKILL partly as the hook spine; the full 5-part order is NEW.
- **Body:** first body point must blow their mind. Put your SECOND-best point first, best second, then third-best and so on: rising value builds a pattern that keeps them; best-first trains "value is decreasing". Album analogy (the hit single is track 3 or 4, not 1). NEW.
- **Value loop per point:** Context (what it is, simple) > Application (how to do it + examples) > Framing (why it matters and how it fits the whole). Zoom in, zoom out "like a wave". NEW.
- **Transitions:** re-hook between points ("That point was super important, but if you don't also couple it with this one, then the magic is lost").
- **Outro:** end on a high note, summarize, remind them of the pain solved ("free chocolate with the check"). Drives likes, shares, rewatches.
- **Native embeds (CTA without an ad feel):** at outline stage pick the point your free resource naturally solves; frame the pain, offer the resource as the solve, move on. Or speed-run a deep framework and offer the resource as the recap. NEW.

### 2.8 Illusion of novelty (LvuoNlYRs7g). NEW (for "boring" niches)
1. **New reveal + outcome mapping:** reveal a new aspect of an old thing (new frame, name, update, finding) + tie it to an outcome they want. Formula: "reveal new aspect of a thing plus tie it to an outcome you know the viewer wants." Templates: "X new thing just changed the way you get Y result" / "You've probably heard of X topic, well Y just happened that changed the way you do X to get Z result." Water example: timing, not amount, gives "more energy than your morning cup of coffee". "The new reveal makes them look, but the outcome mapping makes them stay."
2. **Contrast framing:** put the new angle right next to the old belief, as a TRUE opposite (a random old thing = confusion). Ask: "What does my audience already believe about this topic that I can hold constant?" Templates: "Everyone thinks X, but actually it's Y." / "You've been told X your whole life. Turns out X is wrong. Y is actually the thing if you want to get Z result." / "X used to be the gold standard, but now Y makes X obsolete."
3. **Urgency (optional "turbo button"; recency bias):** only if real. "This thing just happened a few weeks ago." "This only became possible a couple days ago." "For years, this didn't work until this one change." "This window won't stay open forever." Fake urgency = lost trust.
4. **Bullseye proof / trust ladder:** top = a one-to-one mimic of the viewer (same age, look, problem, situation), or you if you are the same demo; middle = "warm crowd" (a group of people like them got it); bottom = third-party (study, article, stat, award). Climb as high as you honestly can. Example: "I've got a buddy, same age as you, crashes at work every day at 3 p.m..." beats "A study claims...".
5. **Protect the illusion:** don't show the mascot (no hedging, no "this is old news") + gossip-whisperer delivery.
Full example (dentist, root canal) assembled, word for word: "Most people have no idea this exists, but there's a new way to get a robotic root canal with zero needles, zero pain, and you'll be back to work in less than 90 minutes. If you've heard about root canals before, you probably know how painful and uncomfortable they usually are. But that was the old way. This way makes the pain and waiting basically obsolete. Now, the funny thing is almost no dentist has been fully trained on this technique yet... We just had a patient who's terrified of needles and put this off for 2 years that was back to work within the hour..." (He says "assume this is true"; it is a demo of shape, not of facts.)
Measuring it: rank your videos since you started using it, compare winners vs losers on how you did the reveal, proof, urgency (manually or via AI on exported transcripts). "The framework fits one-size-fits-all, the execution will not." His one-on-one clients include "boring" niches: digestive health, B2B sales, fitness, cleaning.

### 2.9 Idea mining (pirnGDCZD3Y)
- **7 idea Legos:** topic, angle (premise + take + key facts), hook (spoken, text, visual), story structure, visual format (layout), key visuals (A-roll, B-roll, animation), audio. Skill uses 11 bricks / 6 bricks; this is a third list. Same idea.
- **Outlier score** = video views / channel average. Filters he uses: outlier 5x+, engagement rate 5%+ (screens out paid brand deals), date window 7/14 days for news, 3/6/12 months for evergreen; sort by outlier score. Engagement filter is NEW.
- **Where to look (2x2):** best = same content type (short vertical video) on the same platform OR other platforms (IG, TikTok, YouTube Shorts). Tweets, blogs, long-form give only topic/angle; hook, structure and format don't carry over, so you "crack the full code" alone. IN SKILL roughly (pipe order).
- **Finding watchlist accounts:** your existing list; one great account > who they follow; platform keyword search > creator > who they follow; database search. 20 to 30 accounts per niche list.
- **Analysis doc:** top section "daily inspiration" (one bullet per video: topic, angle, hook notes, key visuals, link, audio notes); bottom section "long-term patterns" (hook structures, series/story structures, visual formats, tracked over a week or more). First filter: "is this topic interesting to me or dumb?" Then: what worked, what was weak that I can beat. 20 to 30 videos per session. NEW.
- **Remix rule:** hold the winning bricks, remix the rest ("hold 2, remix 5", five-card poker). His habit: usually CHANGE angle, hook, visual format; usually KEEP topic, some key visuals, audio mood. See section 5 for the conflict with the skill.

## 3. Application to a business coach's talking-head reel

Placeholders in [brackets]; never fill them with invented numbers.

**Pre-write (10 min, Sam with client):**
1. Pick one buyer pain only that coach's buyer has (avatar framing). Source: the coach's sales-call questions and comments.
2. Write the last line first (the "last dab": a quotable rule that also loops into line one).
3. Bullet 3 to 5 facts or takes; score each with the shock test (how many of 100 of their buyers have NOT heard it). Keep the top 2 or 3. If nothing scores high, the idea is weak: don't film it.
4. Name the mechanism (term branding), e.g. the coach's own method name. Write the target emotion at the top (for coaches usually surprise/curiosity or frustration-then-relief).
5. Pick hook format + structure from the coach's niche outliers. For coaches the natural pairs: Problem hook + Problem solver; Contrarian hook + Breakdown; Case study hook + Case study explainer (client result); Personal experience hook + Personal story.

**Reel template (30 to 60 s talking head), built from his pieces:**
- 0 to 3 s, WHAT + WHY (jumbled W's fixed), negative or contrast frame, buyer words. "Most [sales] coaches lose the deal in the first five minutes of the call, not at the close." Text hook = same meaning; a visual under it (e.g. a call screen, a calendar of no-shows).
- Common belief, out loud: "You've been told the close is where you win or lose." (contrast framing, true opposite)
- Contrarian + named mechanism: "Turns out the close is decided by what I call the [Name] check, and it happens before you pitch anything."
- Proof, highest honest rung: client like the viewer ("[Client], [same niche], [real result]") > "most of my clients" > outside source. On screen: the screenshot or number. If no proof exists, demonstrate the skill instead; never invent.
- Point 1 (second-best), value loop: what it is > how (one example from a real call) > why it matters.
- Loop opener with contrast word: "But that only works if you also fix this."
- Point 2 (best) = aha #2. Thought narration once: "Now you're probably thinking, my leads are different."
- Aligned keyword CTA right after aha #2, tied to the topic (native embed): "Comment [KEYWORD] and I'll send you the [Name] check I use on every call."
- Last line = the quotable rule that loops back to the opening.
- Language pass: remove if/maybe/might/probably/could; "when you do this", "the reason this works". Every beat joined by but/therefore. Jagged line lengths. Delivered like telling one friend a secret, not a hype announcement.

**Story reel variant (client win or own failure), addiction loop:**
Stakes: "[Client] had [real deadline] to [real risk]." Big question with enough specifics to guess: "On our second call she told me one thing that changed the whole plan." Head fake: the answer contradicts what a coach would predict but makes sense instantly. Re-hook: "which would've been great, except..." Then lesson + CTA.

**Ideas for coaches ("boring niche" fix):** illusion of novelty: take an evergreen topic (objection handling, follow-up, niching) > find the new angle or name > put it against the belief everyone in the niche repeats > tie to the outcome (booked calls, closed deals) > honest proof > don't add "of course this is classic SPIN selling".

## 5. Contradictions and changes

1. **Remix amount.** Ideas video: "hold 2, remix 5"; he usually changes angle, hook AND visual format. Skill (from another Kallaway video): change 1 to 3 bricks and it must improve something. Not a real conflict on "improve", but on how much to change. Suggest: keep the skill's 1 to 3 for clients (lower risk), note his heavier remix as his own habit. Flag for Sam.
2. **Re-hook cadence.** Story locks: every 20 to 30 s in shorts, 60 to 90 s long-form. Skill: something resolves every 15 to 25 s. 7 mistakes: peaks every 2 to 5 min and "5-minute increments" (long-form). Addiction loop: "no gap" at all. These are different scales, not one number. Ruling suggestion: in a 30 to 60 s reel, at least one loop opener between each section; no dead air at section ends.
3. **Where to put the best stuff.** 7I50: second-best point first, best second. W--tE: two ahas as early as possible. Modern arc: biggest peak can be first or last. Compatible: strong early (first point must blow their mind), but not best-then-decline.
4. **Intro order.** 7I50 (older) intro: context, common belief, contrarian, proof + plan. Broad-to-narrow (newer): context before stakes. Jumbled W's: what + why first. Addiction loop: stakes before the story. They agree on "say WHAT it's about first", they differ on whether stakes come in line one. For reels: what + why (topic + stake) in the first sentence.
5. **Delivery energy.** Skill: give 10 to 20% more energy on camera. Illusion of novelty: "gossip whisperer" beats "town crier" (lower voice, secret tone). Embedded truths: sound certain. Combined: energy and certainty, not hype. Flag for Sam.
6. **Metaphors.** Mistake 6 says re-explain hard points with a metaphor "like to a child". Sam's skill bans invented metaphors (use real cases). Use a real example in that second pass, not a metaphor.
7. **Illusion of novelty vs honesty.** He demos with a made-up root canal claim ("assume it's true") and says never reveal that the "new" angle is old. He also says never fake urgency or proof. For Sam's clients: the new NAME and FRAME are fine; the claim, numbers, proof and urgency must be real. Never script "new" for something that isn't, if a buyer could check it.
8. **Same advice, repackaged.** The newest video (WluuZB, 2 weeks) repeats the oldest (t5Z) and KyC8r word for word. No reversals between old and new; the newest adds only the 7 mistakes. The big shift over time is focus: older videos (7I50, _Z11, pirnG) are about views, faster scripting, and his tool; newer ones (W--tE, LvuoN) are about conversion for business owners (avatar framing, proof ladder, double aha, boring niches). That newer set is the closest to Sam's clients.
9. **Thumbnail before script** (long-form only): he disagrees with "experts" who demand title AND thumbnail first; title yes, thumbnail can stay loose.
10. **Claims without data**, do not quote as fact: "visual hooks 10x more effective", "two ahas = 10x trust", "80% bounce before rising action", "lose 50% in the first 30 s", "people twice as motivated to avoid pain" (he cites loss aversion research but shows none), "40M views in 5 days" (his own video, anecdote).
11. **Story structure list:** says 7, names 8 (case study / explainer merged, educational = personal story). Tutorial gets no hook pairing.

## 1. How the algorithm works (per Kallaway)

**The platform's goal.** The algorithm's only job is to keep viewers on the app longer, because more time means more ads. It is "one giant matchmaker" between videos and viewers. If you help it make good matches, it pushes you. (8cQ, h_yQ, 94ez) **NEW**

**"Social media is now just media."** From about 2020, TikTok pushed every platform from showing follower content to matching anyone's content with anyone. Old follower-first strategy is dead. (h_yQ) **NEW**

**Step by step after you post** (8cQ, repeated in 94ez). All **NEW**:
1. **Digital fingerprint / topic mapping.** The platform analyzes the video in several ways at once: computer vision for the picture, audio for the transcript, and metadata (caption, hashtag, creator, location).
   - h_yQ narrows this: targeting is mostly driven by the transcript, then somewhat by the caption.
   - h_yQ adds: it then builds a lookalike audience from whoever engaged.
2. **Fit score.** A prediction of who will like the video.
3. **Initial sample of about 200 people**, the ones with the highest fit score.
   - 8cQ says most of them are non-followers, because the platform is testing how strangers react. That is why followers matter little in sampling.
   - 94ez says "some followers, some non-followers".
   - SJ0 says "two to 300, a mix of followers and non-followers".
4. **Result of the test.**
   - Positive: push to about 2,000, then 20,000, then 200,000, until the data weakens. Even big hits stop when they run out of people who want them.
   - Neutral: re-score and resample about 200 more.
   - Negative: stop almost immediately.
   - "200-view jail" = the first sample group gave bad data.

**What the platform reads from the sample** (8cQ). **NEW**
- (1) Average watch time, with % completion as a proxy.
- (2) Engagement rate = (likes + comments + shares) / views.
- (3) Watch-time session share: the share of a viewer's session spent on your videos. You cannot see this number anywhere, but he calls it critical.

**Two levers to "hijack" it** (8cQ, 94ez). **NEW**
- (a) Help it build the right fit score, so the right 200 people get the video.
- (b) Make those 200 actually watch and engage.

**Lever (a): audience matching.** Make videos about the same topic for the same avatar again and again.
- After a few similar videos, the platform knows "X topic for Y avatar" and reuses the earlier sample groups.
- Mixed topics (tech, then health, then politics) produce a blended fit score. The sample comes back weak and the video flops.
- **"Even one viral hit to an audience outside your core demo results in the next several videos having poor sample data"** (8cQ). **NEW**
- After **30 to 50 videos** on one pain-point-driven topic, "the algorithm will learn ... and start pushing you into that community" (h_yQ).
- Audit: scroll your last 20 videos. Same topic and same avatar but still no views? Then the problem is video quality. All over the place? Then that is the problem (h_yQ).
- Platforms "do not like variety shows" (viT).
- **IN SKILL** in spirit (pick the game, on-target virality). + The mechanism above and the 20-video audit are new.

**Lever (b): the "four horsemen" of engagement** (8cQ). **NEW** as a set (relevance and usefulness are partly in the skill's trust formula).
1. Relevant topic for the ideal viewer.
2. Non-obvious and tactically implementable.
3. High absorption: they actually understand it.
4. Short distance to implement: a little action gives a big result.
- He says the same four make viewers into buyers.

**Comments as a push lever** (8cQ). **NEW**
1. Take a hard stance; hedging kills comments.
2. Pick the contrarian side.
3. Ratchet up the framing ("better than every pasta shop in the world").
4. Build around cult-loved brands, people or movements (Nike, not "shoes").
5. Aim for strong emotion.
- 94ez, viral game only: create controversy so people split for or against. "Light the first match."

**What kills or blunts reach:**
- **Boosting posts / buying views or followers = F tier, "will nuke your channel"** (SJ0). **NEW**
  - Paid followers who don't care fill your next organic sample groups, don't engage, and the sample data dies.
  - Seen in "dozens" of channels. Heavy boosting may mean it is "easier to start over".
  - Ads Manager campaigns that send people to a site are fine; the Boost button and buying followers are not.
- **Links / pushing people off-platform** (URLs in tweets, LinkedIn posts, stories): platforms "nuke your engagement" (h_yQ). **NEW**
  - The only ramp to build is rented to owned (email, community).
  - He does not apply this to comment-keyword DMs. His own funnel uses a ManyChat flow plus a caption keyword (w-Rg, TNEy).
- **Topic sprawl / off-niche virality** (8cQ, h_yQ, viT, DFSU).
- **Too narrow can also under-perform** (94ez, newer). Every video ultra-narrow "makes the algorithm lazy and sleepy". See section 5.
- **What doesn't matter: posting time, hashtags, the captions themselves, settings hacks.** "Fugazi", F tier. "Focus on the cake" (8cQ, SJ0). **NEW**
  - Small tension: h_yQ says the caption feeds topic detection "somewhat". So the caption helps classification but is not a growth hack.

**Expectation anchor (94ez).** In a niche with about 1M possible buyers, "10,000 targeted views on a video is actually extremely good". Compare views apples to apples. **NEW**

**Never too late (viT).** Most creators quit or graduate out, so the pyramid is emptier than it looks, and daily attention hours are rising. **NEW**

## 2. Growth playbook from zero (as he teaches it)

### 2a. The 2026 four-step playbook (o_n5). **NEW** as a structure.

**Step 1. Pick the game.** There are five, and you can't win them all from one channel:
1. Mainstream cult fandom: ad money.
2. Category cult fandom: CPMs and brand deals; you are the product.
3. Intuitive products.
4. Explanatory products.
5. **Authority building**: the goal is fast conversions to email or a low-ticket product, by direct attribution.
- Coaches, and Sam as a service seller, are game 5. **IN SKILL** as "authority building". + The other four games and the stated conversion goal are new.

**Step 2. Build the content data system before posting.**
- **"Callaway's paradox"**: big creators' performance is "compressed". Their duds still do OK and their hits don't break out as high, so you can't learn from them.
- Ignore accounts over 1M. Study micro (10K to 50K), small (50K to 250K) and medium (250K to 1M) accounts.
- **IN SKILL** (10K to 250K band, "face and name"). + The 1M cutoff and the compression explanation are new. w-Rg repeats 10K to 250K: "a 12,000 follower account with an 86x outlier is the purest validated winner".

**Step 3. Experimentation lab.**
- Not married to topic pillars; try what the data says.
- "As much quantity as possible at an 8/10 quality."
- **Batches of 10.** Expect **1 to 3 batches (10 to 30 videos) to find the first 10x+ outlier on a cold channel** if you know what you're doing; slower alone.
- Staffing ratio: 1 operator per 3 editors, 1 editor about 5 videos a week.
- **NEW** (the skill has batches of 10 for analysis, not this lab framing).

**Step 4. Content factory.**
- A winner "graduates" to the factory, which churns slight iterations until it stops working.
- Keep **3 to 4 winner templates** in rotation. Turn the lab back up when one dies.
- **IN SKILL** partly ("triple down", "two hits = a formula"). + The lab/factory dial is new.

**Judge outliers by the right metric.**
- Games 1 and 2: views, follows or engagement.
- **Games 3 to 5: dollars per view (or conversions per view), not views or followers.**
- **IN SKILL** ("conversions per video"). + Dollars ÷ views is the exact apples-to-apples metric.

### 2b. The 100-day lead-machine blueprint (TNEy, his own account)
His results: about 60K followers, 30K emails, $100K+ in 100 days, 85 reels plus 14 carousels, 4 to 5 hours a week. Only 6 videos passed 100K views and none passed 1M, on purpose.

1. **Offer first, work backwards.** The offer tells you the avatar, its real and perceived pains, and the words that already sold. A Target Authority Statement exercise gives topic buckets, power words, core beliefs and objections. Without it he says expect "20 to 50 videos guessing". **IN SKILL** (Target Authority Statement). + The four outputs are new.
2. **Unique positioning angle (UPA).** The viewer remembers "one person per lane". Stack differentiation across seven attributes:
   1. topic selection
   2. substance depth
   3. unique stories
   4. avatar specificity
   5. delivery style
   6. storytelling format
   7. visual format
   - He won on four of them: substance, delivery, visual (split screen, premium motion graphics) and one topic bucket (AI workflows).
   - Map the top 5 to 10 videos of niche leaders across the seven and do what isn't being done.
   - Add **creative constraints** (his: "if I have to leave this chair, I won't make that format"; that cuts layouts from about 30 to 5).
   - Spend 30 to 60 minutes total on steps 1 and 2. Don't procrastinate. **NEW**
3. **The 3-2-1 content mix.** 7 videos a week = 3 topic buckets × 2 videos + 1 "chaos agent" (anything, or a double-down).
   - 1 bucket is broad within the niche (for follower jumps).
   - 2 buckets are narrow, aimed at the buyer.
   - Pick the buckets from data:
     - Take 20 to 25 competitor channels of 10K to 250K, last 3 to 6 months.
     - Set an engagement-rate floor of 2% (this filters out brand deals) and sort by outlier score.
     - Analyze the top 50 to 100 and export.
     - Ask an AI for 1 broad and 2 narrow buckets.
   - Re-evaluate at 2 weeks: kill dead buckets, double down on hot ones.
   - Topics are "the load-bearing element ... everything else is icing".
   - Some topics just don't pull in a niche. In his niche, ideas, strategy, recording, delivery and editing lose; hooks, scripting, storytelling and AI workflows win.
   - **NEW**
4. **The data-driven iteration loop.** Post 7, read the data, use it on the next 7, and keep growing the sample (7, 14, 21...).
   - His example: AI analysis showed **"if-then" hooks ("if you want to grow X, do this") flopped and declarative hooks crushed**. He switched and the videos grew.
   - **IN SKILL** partly (batch analysis every 10; declarative hook rule). + The weekly 7-batch cadence and this specific hook finding are new.
5. **Systems.**
   - Batch 7 to 10 scripts in 3 to 4 hours; record 10 in 45 to 60 minutes.
   - Hire an editor at "$100 to $150 per video" (TNEy). SJ0 says "$50 to $150; less than that you get less quality and taste".
   - Editing is "the main constraint" holding people at 2 to 4 videos a week instead of 7.
   - Idea pipes: own winners remixed; competitor outliers ("do I believe something different?"); scroll reactions; customer conversations.
   - **IN SKILL** (pipes). + The editor price band is new and directly useful for Sam's positioning.
6. **Funnel.**
   - Every video gets a CTA with a lead magnet made for that video.
   - About 1% of viewers gave an email (about 4 to 4.5M views gave about 30K unique emails).
   - 10 to 15K emails a month.
   - **IN SKILL** (funnel). + The ~1% benchmark is new.

### 2c. Quantity vs quality: three phases (DFSU). **NEW**
- **Phase 1, skill acquisition, about 20 to 30 videos.** Quantity over everything, but **one at a time: make one, post it, analyze, learn, make the next. Do NOT batch** (a batch of 20 means you only learn at video 21). Two or three a day is fine.
- **Phase 2, about 20 to 30 videos.** Once skills are about 7/10, set a quality floor. "Quality" means the best you could do on the idea, research, delivery and packaging, not the visuals. Drop to 3 to 5 posts a week and never go below the floor.
- **Phase 3, forever.** Quality about 9/10. Slowly inch volume up: 2, 3 ... 7 a week, then up to 2 to 3 a day.
- "Everyone I've coached who reached million-view videos went through the suck. Everyone who didn't quit during the suck."

### 2d. Other growth rules
- **Volume** (viT). "You're not winning because you're not posting enough." High volume plus improving each rep means you cannot lose. Low volume is a systems problem, not a motivation problem. Fix it in this order: free training, then AI tools, then paid training, then team (outsource editing first). **IN SKILL** ("cannot lose"). + The causes and the fix order are new.
- **Consistency gives a "multiplier" when the audience match is tight** (I4bES). Distracted viewers need more reps of you. **IN SKILL** partly.
- **One platform and one format for 6 months, plus email** (h_yQ, viT bonus 2). Pick where your buyers live; ask a few customers if unsure.
  - On that platform, comment on others in the niche, reply to every comment, and use secondary formats such as Stories.
  - You may cross-post, but don't optimize for other platforms (DFSU). **NEW**
- **Islands, not ecosystems** (h_yQ). Don't force people from Instagram to YouTube; it churns them and platforms punish links. Keep visual consistency so people discover you naturally ("world building"). **NEW**
- **Gather vs go seasons** (viT). Keep research short. In "go" season, mute inputs and only make. **NEW**
- **Info diet** (fVsl). Only 2 to 3 sources per topic, and only people with the outcome you want. **NEW**
- **Bottleneck theory** (fVsl). Ideas (topic + angle) are the highest-leverage part. Spend most time there and split the rest evenly. **IN SKILL** (ideas > hooks).
- **Don't compare yourself to pro teams** (Gary Vee, Bartlett, Hormozi run clipping farms and paid plus organic). Compare to people 3 to 10 steps ahead (viT). **NEW**
- **Anything but quitting** (fVsl). Drop expectations to zero; his rule was "10 years before I need a dollar". **NEW**
- **One by one by one** (fVsl): one traffic source, one avatar, one offer. 2×2×2 = 8 times the complexity. He admits he broke this (tech plus marketing) and is "paying for it dearly". **NEW**

### 2e. Research, outliers and ideas: his process
- **Where:** small channels 10K to 250K (or up to 1M) in your niche and game. See 2a and 2b.
- **Mining:** last 3 to 6 months; engagement-rate floor 2%; sort by outlier score; analyze the top 50 to 100; cluster into topic buckets. Per idea: "do I believe something different than what they said?" (TNEy, tKRD). **IN SKILL** (2-week mining, 50 to 100). + The 2% floor and 3 to 6 month window are new.
- **What to take** (newest, w-Rg): **take only the topic and the power words or phrases.** Do the format, layout, hook, script and take yourself, and put them into YOUR proven format. Don't keep 80% and change 20%; "that worked 2 years ago" and now leads to mid-slop. **CONFLICTS WITH SKILL** (the skill says remix by holding most bricks and changing 1 to 3). See section 5.
- **Making the angle** (tKRD): data picks the topic. Then ask "what's the one or two things I give my clients on this that they're happiest about?" That answer is the angle; what you say is the substance. Idea = topic + angle + substance. **IN SKILL** (topic, seed, substance). + The client-based angle question is new.
- **Contrarian insight** (fVsl, DFSU): list the viewer's common beliefs, write why each is wrong and what's true instead. Or ask: "what do I believe that 99% haven't heard or would disagree with?" **IN SKILL** (running list of contrarian takes).
- **Audience of one** (DFSU): write for one named person who is a proxy for the buyer. **NEW**
- **Data-enabled creativity** (DFSU, SJ0): "data aims, creativity digs". All data trends to average; all gut means starving artist. **NEW** as a framing.

## 3. Best vs worst advice, and unpopular tips

**SJ0 tier list (about 1 month old):**
- **S tier**
  - Use data to guide strategy. Organic is "paid ads you don't pay for": test, then double down on winners.
  - Focus on idea = topic + angle, above hooks.
- **A tier**
  - Give everything away free and sell implementation. Works for service and agency models, not info-only.
  - Hire an editor ASAP. He edited his first 150 himself at 4 to 6 hours each and calls that a mistake.
- **B tier:** "Hooks are the most important thing." Good, but second or third after the idea.
- **C tier**
  - Jump on trending formats. Use one only if it helps the viewer understand; the ranking/tier format is good, the "Kumar method" edit format was not.
  - More time per video always performs better. Return on time is a bell curve; he spends 30 to 45 minutes of his own time per rep.
- **D tier**
  - "More views = more money."
  - "Pick a niche with less competition." Exception: an emerging category, for the fame game. Otherwise pick where you have skill and an offer.
  - "You are the niche."
- **F tier**
  - Boost your best videos.
  - Copy what works word for word ("you are an imposter").
  - "Don't use CTAs." He changed his mind on this in the last 6 months; it is now a CTA on every video.
  - Hashtags, posting times, settings hacks.
- Tags: **NEW** as a list. Partly IN SKILL (ideas > hooks, CTA every reel, 4 S's).

**DFSU's "biggest lies":**
1. "There's a set formula." The only universal law is **time under trust** (minutes watched above a trust line). **IN SKILL** (trust = minutes × relevance × usefulness).
2. "You are the niche." Don't document your life, build in public or flex hobbies.
3. "Your hooks or storytelling are why videos flop." It's only the topic, or a take that isn't new. **IN SKILL**.
4. "Data kills creativity."
5. "More views = more money." Offer/audience alignment is the difference between $5K and $500K months.
6. "Create for everyone in your niche." Use an audience of one.
7. Quantity vs quality: see the three phases in 2c.
8. "Be on every platform."
9. "Jump on trends." Stop doing trends, trend hooks, trend music and trend formats; start them instead.

**viT "brutal truths":**
- You-are-the-niche is "hard mode".
- Be a scientist, not an artist.
- It's never too late.
- You're not posting enough.
- Make more, watch less.
- Bonus: don't compare to pro teams; one platform and one format for 6 months; people buy on trust (**$1,000 trust test**: an ICP viewer watches, then says what problem it solves and whether they'd pay $1,000 to fix it; a mismatch means unclear messaging); **talk slightly faster** (average consumption speed keeps creeping up); ideas always win (spend the most time on ideas, the least on the edit).
- Tags: NEW, except "ideas win" (IN SKILL).

**w-Rg "unpopular tips" (about 7 days old). All NEW unless marked:**
1. **One or two formats, hammered for hundreds of reps.** Viewers don't get bored because they see hundreds of other videos between yours. Familiarity makes them lock in. It's the creator who gets bored. Run the channel like a TV show (Survivor, The Office).
2. **AI for input, not output.** Use AI for strategy, research, topics and analysis. Keep scripting, recording and editing human for now. AI-edited videos "look generic"; simple talking-head edits can get away with it, cinematic or motion-heavy edits can't.
3. **Be better, stay better.** The average quality in every niche drifts up (example: from 3/10 to 6/10 in 12 months). A 5/10 creator who was winning falls below the line. That explains "it worked 3 to 6 months ago and then stopped without me changing anything." The fix is to raise skill to 7 to 8, eventually 10.
4. **Personality / "connection" content is not needed for authority sellers.** Agitate a pain, offer a solution, get out of the way. Buyers buy on proof of results with a near-identical avatar.
5. Study 10K to 250K accounts. **IN SKILL**.
6. **A value-first native-embed CTA on every video, carousel and YouTube video.** "In 2026 brains are mashed potatoes." Each video is a "sniper shot" to extract an email; don't count on long-term trust building up. Always have a ManyChat flow running and the keyword in the caption. **IN SKILL** (CTA every reel). + The "sniper shot" reason is new.
7. **Don't chase virality; serve an underserved avatar slice consistently.** Chris case: restaurant owner using AI, about 2.8 to 3K followers, a grainy unscripted car video at about 10 to 12K views sold out his capacity. "Meta's targeting is so good it will find those people."
8. **Copying.** Re-uploading or saying the same words = "scumbag". Take only topic and power words (see 2e).

**tKRD (about 18 hours old, newest):**
1. **Visual moat.** AI editing with Claude or ChatGPT is about to flood the feed with "the same look and feel". He says build a signature visual style across 6 dimensions:
   1. character: you, what you wear, where you sit, shot angle
   2. set: background, colors, objects, mood
   3. visual selection: graphics, B-roll
   4. layout
   5. branding and typography
   6. motion effects
   - Map these inside your niche (what not to copy) and outside it ("transpositioning").
   - "Maybe visuals didn't matter in 2021; in 2026 they're one of the most important differentiators." **NEW**
2. **Delivery cadence.** Stress the power words, keep sentences short and punchy, avoid run-ons ("Pac-Man eating dots"). Drill it with "copy speech": imitate a creator whose cadence you like, reading their transcript out loud. **NEW** (the skill has the +10 to 20% energy direction, not cadence).
3. **Anti-slop protocol.** Don't use AI to edit, record or write your words. Use AI only where data frees time for the human part. Feeds are converging to one cluster; break out to the edges. **NEW**
4. **Idea quality.** "If your content isn't cutting through, it is always an idea problem." The topic, the take or the substance is weak, or the substance doesn't clearly support the take. **IN SKILL**.
5. **Double volume while keeping taste.** Use a hybrid AI system: AI for research, human for angle and script. Batch 7 to 10 scripts in 3 to 4 hours, record 7 in 1 hour, hand off editing. This took him from 3 to 7 a week; his marketing IG went from 0 to 160K in 6 months on about 5 hours a week. **NEW**
6. **4 S's of hooks:** Subject (in sentence one; hiding it frustrates), **Stakes (the one most often missing)**, **Simple** (third-grade words in the first 1 to 2 sentences), Super clear (short, punchy, active). **IN SKILL**, but the skill's third S is "Speed", from SJ0. This newest version uses "Simple"; speed is folded into "super clear".

**Other items in the older videos:**
- **I4bES (2025): retention editing is dead.** Flashbangs, rapid cuts, screaming colors: "the coconut shell" blocks the value. Casual edits win; the value inside must now be real. Get a skill and proof before making content (he did this himself). **NEW**. Contradicted later; see section 5.
- **94ez: substance "one click above" the buyer's sophistication.** A 2 to 3/10 buyer gets 4 to 5/10 content. Going over beginners' heads is fine if they aren't buyers. **NEW**
- **94ez: formats that signal trust by default.** A surgeon wears the lab coat in the office; an accountant sits at the desk. Film in 4K with dialed audio, especially for high-ticket and unknown sellers ("signals that you care"). Avoid "junk food formats" with no authority. **NEW**
- **94ez, viral game only:**
  - Broaden the topic's audience size.
  - Novel angle.
  - Proper nouns with built-in meaning ("the robot UFC").
  - Comment controversy.
  - Either raw format with perfect emotion, or top 0.1% visuals, to start a share loop.
  - "Specificity drives dollars, broadness drives virality. Those are at odds." **NEW**
- **h_yQ: value doesn't accrue at the media layer.** AdSense and brand deals pay little; the money is in your own offer. **NEW**

## 4. Application

### 4a. A business-coach client (talking-head reels for leads)
Only Kallaway-backed moves:
1. **Game 5 (authority building).** Judge videos by leads (or dollars) per view, not views (o_n5, TNEy). Anchor expectations: in a niche, about 10K targeted views is strong (94ez). The Chris case shows about 10K views can fill capacity (w-Rg).
2. **Offer before content** (TNEy). Pull from the coach's offer: avatar, real and perceived pains, sales-call words, core beliefs, objections. Then pick 3 buckets using the 3-2-1 mix (1 broad within coaching, 2 narrow on the buyer's pain), chosen from 10K to 250K coach accounts with outlier sort and the 2% engagement-rate floor. Re-check at 2 weeks.
3. **Stay on one topic and avatar.** No lifestyle or "you are the niche" posts. No off-niche viral grabs; one off-niche hit poisons the next several sample groups (8cQ). But don't make every video ultra-narrow; mix broad and narrow (94ez).
4. **Trust signals in the format** (94ez). Real setting (desk, office, client context), clean 4K, dialed audio. Substance one click above the buyer's level. Proof from the same avatar beats personality (w-Rg). Run the $1,000 trust test on a finished reel (viT).
5. **Every reel: a native-embed CTA.** Agitate the pain, offer a free, matching resource by comment keyword, keep a ManyChat flow live, put the keyword in the caption (SJ0, w-Rg, TNEy). Benchmark: he gets about 1% of viewers as emails (TNEy). Do not use the Boost button on reels (SJ0). Paid ads via Ads Manager to a landing page are fine.
6. **Volume through systems.** Batch script and record (7 to 10 in about an hour); the coach never edits. Target up to 7 a week (TNEy, tKRD). Use one or two proven formats as a "TV show" (w-Rg).
7. **Hooks.** Declarative, not "if you want X, then..." (TNEy). Run the 4 S's with stakes first (tKRD).
8. **Delivery.** Slightly faster pace (viT). Cadence with stress on power words; copy-speech drill (tKRD).
9. **Editing direction for Sam as the coach's editor.**
   - Newest Kallaway: a signature visual style is the 2026 differentiator, because AI-edited reels are converging (tKRD).
   - He also says avoid noisy retention tricks that bury the value (I4bES, older).
   - Combined: a distinct, consistent visual identity, not loud effects.
   - The "one or two formats hammered" rule (w-Rg) means building a repeatable template per client.
10. **If a client says "it worked 3 to 6 months ago and stopped"**, it's the quality-drift effect (w-Rg). Raise the idea and execution level; don't change hacks.

### 4b. Sam's own editor account (samvexedits: 7 followers, before/after reels at 3 to 50 views)
What Kallaway's content does and doesn't cover:
- **No video addresses results BELOW the ~200 first-test sample** (3 to 50 views), or brand-new accounts with under 10 followers. **Not covered; don't invent a reason.** The closest he says is that bad first-sample data makes it "stop pushing almost immediately" (8cQ). That fits a test that dies early but doesn't explain the size.
- **He says nothing specific about editor portfolios or before/after formats.**

What his framework would point at (inferred from his rules, not quoted about Sam):
1. **Which game, which avatar?** Before/after edit reels are a format, not a topic for a buyer. If Sam wants clients (coaches), that's game 5, and the buyer is a coach, not other editors. Editors with 1K to 17K views may be playing game 2 (category fandom for editors) or a different avatar. Per 94ez and o_n5, decide the game first; compare views apples to apples within it.

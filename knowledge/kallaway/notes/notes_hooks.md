## 1. Core principles

**P1. A hook has to create a "freeze point" by setting off an orienting response.** NEW (the mechanism; the 4 S's are ALREADY)
- Why: the feed is a highway, thumb moving, eyes glazed. To stop the thumb for even 1 second you need the involuntary "wait, what was that?" reaction, like seeing a wolf in your backyard. To get it the hook must answer two questions in the viewer's head: *what is this about* and *what do I get if I keep watching* (subject + outcome). The 4 S's (Subject, Stakes, Speed, Super clear) build those two answers.
- Adds to the skill: Speed is "compression, not velocity". Rushing the delivery is just as bad: "an unabsorbed hook is just as bad as no hook." For education content, stakes = the outcome the viewer wants (views, customers, money, time back with family).
- Source: pNIYikmYsyw, opening section.

**P2. A hook's only job is opt-in: give topic clarity + ON-TARGET curiosity.** ALREADY (clarity + "for them")
- Adds: he calls it "on-target curiosity" = the viewer believes the topic is for them AND is curious. He says on purpose that he does NOT want to trick off-target people into watching: "if I'm talking about investing and you don't care about investing I actually don't want to trick you into watching" (LmXp). The newest video says the hook "aims the viewer so they can decide to opt in or out" (vmsD).
- Source: 2byPP_9F0-Q opening; LmXpbP7dD48 step 1; vmsDhY9ITlI.

**P3. Four hook mistakes, diagnosed in order: Delay, Confusion, Irrelevance, Disinterest.** ALREADY (order is in section 3)
- Adds examples and fixes he gives:
  - Delay: in a 13-line script, if line 3 is the first time the topic appears, cut lines 1 and 2 ("speed to value"). Bad: "Guys, this is one of the craziest things I've ever seen. And when you see it, you're never going to believe it." Good: "If you have gut issues, these three remedies will help you immediately." Vague hooks only work on TikTok because of the creator's looks, facial emotion or text hook. "The spoken words actually add zero value." Retention in the first 2 seconds "falls off like a cliff".
  - Confusion: bad "These guys built a $30 million empire and the online money they made is most difficult to earn if you don't develop a journaling practice like they did." Good "These guys built a $30 million empire and their secret for earning money online was their insane journaling practice." Fixes: fewer words, sixth-grade reading level, active voice ("the dog jumped", not "the jump of the dog"). Test: "Is there more than one way these sentences could be interpreted?" Prompt he gives: "I've written a hook for a short form video about X topic. I need help increasing the clarity and the framing of the sentences I used. I want the meaning to be the exact same, but can you rewrite this in a sixth grade reading level so that there's no misunderstanding from the viewer?"
  - Irrelevance: two fixes. (a) "you/your" not "I/me": "I've struggled with skin problems my whole life" makes the viewer ask whether they are like you; "If you've struggled with skin problems your whole life…" doesn't. (b) Expected value: frame it as a need-to-have pain, not a nice-to-have: "these are three common trends in skincare" (nice to have) vs "if you struggle with acne, try these three things" (need to have).
  - Disinterest: open a curiosity loop with contrast (see P5).
- Source: 2byPP_9F0-Q (whole video).

**P4. The 3-step spoken hook formula: Context Lean → Scroll-Stop Interjection → Contrarian Snapback.** ALREADY (the "spine"), adds details:
- Context lean has two jobs: topic clarity, plus getting them to lean in. The four ways to make them lean in: establish common ground, reference a benefit or pain point, use a metaphor to simplify a complex idea, or say something that blows their mind.
- Interjection = "a stun gun", a single setup line built on a contrast word (but, however, yet, although, therefore, on the other hand). Snapback = "goes in the opposite direction of the initial lean… still on topic… the bigger the shock the bigger the snap."
- Example (Vegas Sphere, 8M views): "The tech in the Vegas sphere is insane. Biggest screen ever built, 20 times bigger than an IMAX. But get this, the screen is actually the least impressive part of the whole thing, because the most impressive part is the audio."
- Business example he gives (real estate, lead-gen): "There are three massive mistakes people are making with their mortgage. The average person pays an extra $122,000 per year. Now most people think it's because of high interest rates, but it turns out it has nothing to do with that, because the biggest waste of money is actually coming from…" (he says he invented the content; the structure is the point).
- Short form: "If you want to achieve X, don't do Y, do Z." The warning: "you need Z to actually be compelling… if you don't actually have anything at the end of the rainbow, people would just churn and you're a fraud."
- Source: LmXpbP7dD48, tip 1.

**P5. Curiosity = contrast (A vs B), stated or implied.** ALREADY. Adds:
- Why: B (your alternative) re-triggers the "sticky pain point" from A not solving their problem, so the comparison reagitates the pain. B has to solve the pain "faster, better, or cheaper".
- Stated: "Most people solve their acne with Accutane, but I have an herbal remedy that does it three times faster." Implied: "If you want to solve your acne, this herbal supplement is eight times more effective" (the baseline is understood).
- A curiosity loop, as he defines it: see something, ask a question, get context that answers it, which opens a new question, and so on.
- Hooks are really 2 to 3 lines: clarity comes in line one, contrast in the next one or two. If you can do both in one line, "those are the easiest hooks that you can reuse."
- Source: 2byPP_9F0-Q (disinterest section); vmsDhY9ITlI method 3.

**P6. Triple hook, all three aligned (text, visual, spoken), plus a 4th: the caption hook.** ALREADY (triple alignment). NEW: the caption hook = the first line of the post caption above the fold, which "does influence subliminally if someone sticks on the video or not" (he does not go deeper). He also says this is the one thing his 1:1 business-owner clients "just get wrong every time: they work on the spoken hook, but they don't align all three."
- Source: pNIYikmYsyw.

**P7. Text hook is the most important part; the visual hook is the least important (newest view).** PARTLY ALREADY (the skill ranks TEXT > VISUAL > SPOKEN; see section 5, contradiction C1)
- Why text: "the viewers' eyes hunt for the text and can identify power phrases and keywords faster than they can visually comprehend the image."
- Why visual is last: "you can win big with just text hooks and spoken hooks if they're dialed in. And proof of that is all of the Yappers." A visual adds comprehension when it is aligned.
- Source: pNIYikmYsyw.

**P8. Text hook copywriting = subject repeater + ONE of: pain point reminder / desired outcome teaser / future state change.** ALREADY. Adds his examples and the rules he states:
- Kat EBT: "People-pleasing" (subject + pain). Mad GPT: "ChatGPT will sell things for you" (subject + outcome by proxy). His own: "Claude just changed social media forever" (subject + state change). "New storytelling method for modern virality" (state change). "Make your hooks unskippable" (dream outcome).
- Capitalized names and terms are "attention magnets".
- Specificity: "22 days" beats "a few weeks"; "$2,700" beats "a few thousand"; "I made $97,000 in 13 days on Instagram" beats "several thousand dollars in a few weeks".
- Max 2 lines in system font, hard cutoff. 3 lines only with premium type where one line dominates. Break the line where the phrase breaks: "ChatGPT just pioneered / the future of work". No punctuation except quotes and parentheses; no and/or/that/but/therefore. Hold about 3 seconds (the eye goes face → text → visual → text again → decides).
- Design: system font is usually SEPARATE from the word-for-word captions; premium typography can be the word-for-word line. Place it about a third down, or nearer the middle depending on framing. Above the head if the face fills the frame; never cover the eyes or mouth.
- To set the style: scan the top 15 to 20 creators in the niche (30 to 50 of their top videos) for a lay of the land, then remix. Do not copy.
- NEW vs older advice: LmXp (old) said "put three to five words on the screen in a big bold font." His example: in "life-size floor plans" (15M views) he led with the text "future of home design", not the product name, "because life-size floor plans is kind of the name of the thing and I didn't want to risk misunderstanding." In other words, lead with the plain category, not the unfamiliar product name.
- Source: pNIYikmYsyw; LmXpbP7dD48 tip 2.

**P9. Power words / power phrases: level 2 of hook writing (format remixing is level 1).** NEW (the skill only mentions "power words and 1 to 3 word bundles")
- What: 1 to 3 word bundles that amplify the outcome or pain and are "heat-seeking missiles for attention." They squeeze a full sentence into 2 to 3 words.
- How he finds them: list your own top hooks (and competitors') sorted by views, then scan for repeated phrases. His examples: "social media machine" (remix: email marketing machine, LinkedIn machine, sales machine); "scientifically impossible to skip" showed up in 2 of his top 4 hooks, which he calls double validation. Remix it, don't rip it: "scientifically impossible to ignore", "neurochemically impossible to skip", "psychologically impossible to resist".
- The method: find winning formats and twist them, find power words and twist them, then combine the twisted versions. "You're not copy-pasting what's already worked. That's corny and lame."
- Winning format example from his own data: "Here's how to [bigger task] in [shorter time]" ("plan your next 30 days of content in 30 minutes").
- There are "about a dozen" hook formats that outperform (question, tutorial, problem-solution…). His own best types: tutorial, secret reveal/breakdown, problem.
- Source: pNIYikmYsyw (spoken hook section).

**P10. Visual hooks: five categories; for the 80/20, add a small move.** NEW (the skill has the principle but not the categories)
- Categories: (1) graphic/text overlays on a base visual; (2) pattern interrupt / visual switching (cut from you to something else, the "stun gun"); (3) subject motion (you move in or out of frame); (4) visual effect or transition; (5) visual selection (choose a striking shot).
- 80/20: "Move your body in the frame. Move the visual in the frame. Or just move text on top or within the visual." "Some little move, a little lean in, some little arm movement, pull your hair up… doesn't have to be crazy." Overdoing it looks gimmicky.
- Motion amount (older video): "too much motion and you're going to overwhelm them… too little motion and you're going to bore them." Like a deer catching movement out of the corner of its eye. Example: Colin and Samir's Ray-Ban Meta video (60M views) opens with "the perfect amount of rapid motion."
- Source: pNIYikmYsyw; LmXpbP7dD48 tip 2.

**P11. Lead with the benefit or pain, then name the thing.** ALREADY ("pain or benefit before the topic word"). His example: "You should be taking magnesium because it's one of 21 core building block minerals" (weak) vs "If you want better sleep you need to be taking magnesium" (strong): "even if someone is against supplements… they will likely wait and hear you out."
- Source: LmXpbP7dD48 tip 3.

**P12. Wrap an unknown idea in something known ("cult hopping").** NEW, but it CONFLICTS with Sam's voice rule
- Why: unfamiliar words make people "feel dumb… so you bounce." Use a known brand, celebrity, movement or cultural reference as a comparison. His example: tax planning framed as how Taylor Swift's financial advisor would plan her estate around her tour earnings.
- Sam's skill bans invented metaphors and hypothetical celebrity scenarios. The usable part for Sam: reference a REAL known name the audience recognizes (a tool, a famous coach, a known business), never a made-up scenario. UNRESOLVED; Sam decides.
- Source: LmXpbP7dD48 tip 4.

**P13. Speed to value: a timer of about 4 seconds on short form.** PARTLY ALREADY. NEW: the number. "For short form that timer is about 4 seconds; for YouTube videos that timer is maybe 1 to 2 minutes." Give a small hit of value in the hook or right after it: "anything you make after they click off might as well be a black screen." Then repeat the pattern: give context, give value, give context, give value.
- Source: LmXpbP7dD48 tip 5.

**P14. Staccato hook sentences: short at the start, longer later.** NEW
- Why: "shorter sentences force you to achieve max clarity" and raise "the density of value per word." Vary rhythm later in the video, but keep the hook staccato.
- Source: LmXpbP7dD48 tip 6. Supported by ZM3elcBE48I: "if you can say it in six words instead of 15, then use six."

**P15. The lock-in zone (the 1 to 3 sentences right after the hook) matters as much as the hook, maybe more.** ALREADY (seconds 3 to 15, confirm the claim + earn trust, four levers). The newest video adds a whole toolkit, all NEW:
- Definition (newest): "the hook is the first sentence you say and the two to three seconds that you show on screen visually"; the lock-in zone is "the one to three sentences that come directly after the hook." The hook takes the viewer "from zero to one curious", the lock-in from "1 to 10", "like a ratchet on curiosity." Not every video uses one, but per his Sandcastles data, using one of six methods "will dramatically increase the performance."
- The six lock-in methods, each with its "use it when" (vmsDhY9ITlI):
  1. **Tease the proof**: the hook makes a bold claim tied to the outcome; the next line gives concrete numbers or results. His: "You can now have Claude optimize the CTAs in your videos. I ran this one time and it increased my leads per video by 4x." Minnow Lee: "…six viral hooks in 60 seconds. And the last one got me 30 million views and 100,000 followers." Use only if the proof is real; fits authority-building edu channels showing their own or client results.
  2. **Tease a favorable transformation/effect**: use when there is no numeric proof but the wanted change is obvious (for example, confidence). His: "Here's how to write a hook so good it is basically unskippable. This is called the desire hook method. And if you do this right, it'll turn viewers into buyers at a crazy rate." (He says proof would have been better; the video still got "a couple hundred thousand views".)
  3. **Rapidly introduce contrast**: state the common belief, then the alternative. His: "The reason your videos don't hold attention is because you're using the wrong story arc. This is what we were all taught in school… [5 acts]… most people scroll away before the rising action ever gets good. So instead… the modern story arc." Use when the hook's premise is "kind of mid on its own."
  4. **Frame the magic box**: give the method a name the viewer does not know yet and explain it later. His: "…This is called the dopamine addiction loop. It's only four steps, but it uses the exact same psychology casinos use to keep people gambling." This is his go-to, and it stacks most easily with the other five.
  5. **Objection handling**: kill the top reason they would scroll. His: "Everybody's loving yaps right now because they seem so easy to make. But the irony is all the best yaps that go viral are following a set formula" (it heads off "I'm not interesting / no personality"). Jaden: "four YouTube videos that you must watch… And here's the catch. None of these have anything to do with social media" (it heads off "I've seen these lists before"). Use only when there is an obvious objection.
  6. **Bold claim double-down**: say the load-bearing claim again in other words, so they get two chances at clarity. His: "…even if you talk about super boring topics. It's called the illusion of novelty… the ultimate storytelling hack for boring businesses" ("boring was load-bearing"). His F1 one: "those cars don't have a human driver… This is the Abu Dhabi Autonomous Racing League… the cars drive themselves." Use when the claim is sensational but new or unclear.
- What the lock-in does psychologically (pNIYikmYsyw, ALREADY): (1) confirm the hook claim (no bait and switch), (2) rapid trust evaluation. pNIY gives the length as "the next five to 10 seconds… the next two to four sentences."
- Source: vmsDhY9ITlI (all); pNIYikmYsyw (bonus section).

**P16. "One standard deviation away": hook the adjacent tactical pain, not the core pain.** NEW
- Why: core pains are money, time, health and access ("the four horsemen of pain"). Saying "if you want to make more money do this" trips the viewer's "BS detector" ("it can't be that easy"). Hook the tactical pain they believe leads to the core one, and they make the leap themselves.
- His data point: "anytime I talk specifically about making more money those videos don't perform very well, but anytime I talk about specifics of making better videos… those videos crush."
- Source: g_6TIu7zHDw, checkpoint 1.

**P17. Pain acceptance comes first: twist the knife, an audience of one, and the pain-solution gap.** PARTLY ALREADY (you/your). NEW parts:
- Twist the knife: name the frustration bluntly ("if you've been struggling with storytelling, I've been there, I know how hard it is"). FOMO and jealousy deepen it. His hook: "this is the biggest secret weapon used by all of your favorite creators" (jealousy, with the pain implied by the word "creators").
- Audience of one: in his hook's first half he used "you" nine times and "I" zero times, on purpose. "Nobody wants a preacher, they want a friend."
- Pain-solution gap: show the distance between where they are and where they want to be, physically too: moving your hands from one side to the other, or graphics.
- His long-form hook got pain, trust and plan done "in 24 seconds across 12 sentences" (YouTube long-form, not reels).
- Why: "human brains are problem-solving machines"; giving them the problem first points their attention at it.
- Source: g_6TIu7zHDw, checkpoint 1.

**P18. Fear drives purchase; hide it inside a curiosity loop, and be more explicit on short form.** NEW
- Why: of all emotions, fear (of missing out, losing something, not being enough) is the one that most triggers purchase. Saying it outright ("you're going to get fired") kills trust, and lack of trust is "the only thing that can counteract the pull." So show someone relatable going through the fear, or bury it in a curiosity hook ("Nike's being sued for all these runners' injuries").
- Short form exception: "the shorter the medium is, the more explicit you need to be on hammering the fear."
- His conversion hook (Brick, "over $10,000 in sales", 2 hours to make): "Ever feel like your phone controls too much of your life? Well this thing just fixed that. It's called Brick and it changed everything for me." The same hook on the Light Phone got 2.3M views.
- Sequence: establish the fear → tease the solution → amplify the fear → give the solution. Tease the solution early; if you spend 90 of 120 seconds on pain, people leave.
- Source: IywTdYKncqY.

**P19. The best hooks are confident declaratives that front-load the payoff; they don't clear their throat or ask questions.** NEW (these come from the Claude analysis of his and competitors' top hooks, which he endorses on screen)
- "Winners are declarative and front-load the payoff." "Confident declaratives beat hedged questions… winners say things are, losers ask questions." "Avoid throat-clearing openers." "Specificity beats abstraction" (all four channels). "Winners attach a concrete outcome to a constrained input" (30 days of content in 30 minutes). "Two-sentence amplifier": state it, then a second sentence that amplifies (a comparison or contrarian reframe); "losers state the stat and stop." His own: "When Kallaway names a specific outcome the video wins. When he leans on episode branding alone, it sinks 50%." For Peter Visuals: openers that play a reference clip instead of his own framing "underperform by roughly 60%." (The 50% and 60% are the AI's readout on 4 to 5 videos, not his own measurement.)
- His best rewrite in the demo: "Most creators trigger one type of dopamine in their videos. The addictive ones use a six-level ladder."
- Source: a7VjpIqq8Xk.

**P20. Do not copy-paste hooks across niches; remix inside your own lane.** ALREADY (remix, do not invent). Adds: "the psychology doesn't translate. The format crossover doesn't work well… even the grammar usually isn't working." Format fit: a case-study/stat hook template does not fit a concept video. The AI rejected 4 of 5 of Peter Visuals' formats for a psychology topic, and he agreed. Old version (LmXp): "I'm not going to give you a list of 25 proven viral hooks, cuz that's not what you need. What you need is to understand the psychology."
- Source: a7VjpIqq8Xk; LmXpbP7dD48.

**P21. The spoken hook is a delivery problem too: the intro is where you are coldest.** PARTLY ALREADY (10 to 20% more energy, line by line, no dropped ends). NEW parts:
- Throwaway reps: talk for a few minutes before the real take, because "the intro of most videos is the most important part… you're spending the most valuable real estate of the video when you're the coldest and most anxious."
- Downward inflection: end sentences on a falling tone. Upspeak sounds like a question and kills perceived authority. "Same exact words but a completely different level of perceived authority."
- Breathers: a half-second pause after the key word (usually the subject of the sentence) so it lands.
- Dropping phrases: volume and energy fade at the end of sentences. "You can fix volume leveling in post… you can't give yourself more energy in the edit." Fix it by recording line by line.
- Believability: if AI or a ghostwriter wrote it, spend 10 to 20 minutes before recording reworking it into the speaker's own words, or "it feels phony."
- Friend photo: tape a photo of a close friend who is like the ideal viewer under the lens, so the tone becomes conversational.
- Happy to be there: if the speaker does not want to be recording, they shouldn't record; "no amount of editing can fix that likeability in post."
- Copy speech: read along with a creator's transcript while their video plays, to absorb their tempo and emphasis.
- Source: ZM3elcBE48I. g_6TIu7zHDw: "bring your energy of delivery up by like 50%" (see contradiction C5).

**P22. Proof needs to be specific, and visual proof flashed fast is usually enough.** ALREADY (the proof trust anchor). Adds:
- Vague: "I grew my account really fast." Specific: "I grew my account from 2,000 to 47,000 followers over the last 3 months on Instagram with these three methods."
- Trust ladder, best to worst (g_6): (1) proof you solved it for someone just like them (case study, testimonial); (2) proof you did it for yourself; (3) a big following or a co-sign; (4) evidence you were once in their shoes; (5) common ground; (6) look and production quality. "If you have any one of the top three you're probably good"; otherwise stack several of the bottom three. "The bigger the ask the more trust required; the deeper the pain the less trust required."
- Format: screenshots of testimonials, bank statements, A/B tests, before/afters, follower counts. "It usually just takes one or two of these flashed rapidly."
- The 10 to 30 seconds after they accept the pain is the trust window.
- Admission lines ("I hate recording") make the speaker likable. Never invent one.
- Source: ZM3elcBE48I; g_6TIu7zHDw checkpoint 2.

**P23. Name the plan and its structure early ("these are my 5 tips for X").** PARTLY ALREADY (ruling: promise a system once in the intro). Adds why: "people like structure… when they feel smart they associate feeling smart with you." When the number is big, restate it smaller (his "18 hacks" became "six checkpoints" so 18 would not feel overwhelming). Pair the plan with a point of difference: "if everyone says to do Y and you say to do Z…"
- Source: g_6TIu7zHDw checkpoint 3.

## 2. Frameworks, templates and checklists (word for word where he gave them)

**F1. Interrupt Theory (pNIY):** freeze point → orienting response → the hook answers "What is the video about?" + "What will I get if I keep watching?" → built from the 4 S's: Subject, Stakes, Speed, Super clear. "If you miss even one of these, your hook will not hook."

**F2. Four Horsemen hook mistakes (2byPP):** Delay → Confusion → Irrelevance → Disinterest. Fixes: speed to value / sixth-grade, active voice, one interpretation / you-your plus a need-to-have pain / contrast (stated or implied).

**F3. Three-step hook formula (LmXp):** 1 Context Lean (topic clarity + lean-in via common ground, benefit/pain, metaphor, or a mind-blowing fact) → 2 Scroll-Stop Interjection ("but get this", however, yet, although, on the other hand) → 3 Contrarian Snapback. Short form: "If you want to achieve X, don't do Y, do Z."

**F4. Text hook formula (pNIY):** [Subject repeater] + one of [Pain point reminder | Desired outcome teaser | Future state change]. Rules: specific numbers; power words; max 2 lines (3 only with premium type and one dominant line); break at the phrase; no punctuation except quotes and parentheses; no and/or/that/but/therefore; about 3 seconds on screen; about a third down; never over the eyes or mouth.

**F5. Hook-writing levels (pNIY):** Level 1 = format remixing (extract the Mad Lib from winning hooks and swap in your variables). Level 2 = power words (mine repeated phrases from top hooks, twist them with synonyms, combine them with twisted formats). Seed format: "Here's how to [big task] in [short time]."

**F6. Five visual hook categories (pNIY):** overlay, pattern interrupt, subject motion, effect/transition, visual selection. 80/20: move body / move visual / move text.

**F7. Six lock-in zone methods (vmsD):** Tease the proof | Tease a favorable transformation | Rapid contrast | Magic box (named method) | Objection handling | Bold claim double-down. Choose by situation: real numbers → 1; no numbers but an obvious transformation → 2; a dull premise → 3; you have a nameable method → 4 (stacks with any); bold or contrarian with an obvious objection → 5; a new or unclear term → 6.

**F8. Lock-in four levers (pNIY):** looks, packaging (production polish), relatability (the speaker matches what the viewer expects; his example: an accounting creator should be "an old guy with glasses, a button-down shirt who writes on a yellow legal pad"), proof (the "trust anchor", most important).

**F9. Six viewer checkpoints (g_6), the first three happen in the hook:** 1 Pain acceptance (one standard deviation away; twist the knife; audience of one; pain-solution gap) → 2 Trust (6-step trust ladder; flash proof) → 3 Plan of attack (point of difference + a numbered list) → 4 Likability (energy, simplification by saying it twice, be yourself) → 5 Attention (create a hunt, rehook, rhythm) → 6 Action (close the loop, beat expectations, recap).

**F10. Fear-to-purchase framework (Iyw):** 1 Find the target customer → 2 find their biggest collective fears → 3 show that fear coming to life without saying it outright → 4 tease the solution as early as possible → 5 amplify the fear → 6 show the solution.

**F11. The Hook Machine (a7Vj), an AI workflow:** pull the top 10 videos per channel (yours plus competitors), screen out off-topic and paid posts ("any video with below a 2% engagement rate that's a high performer has paid spend behind it"), draw the winner/loser line at the natural view breakpoint, extract per-channel psychology / trigger words / grammar / non-obvious takeaways, merge them across channels into a rubric (his 8 universal principles plus custom ones), then generate hooks per topic using only formats that fit the topic, grade them, and drop anything C or below. Then paste your own draft hook to get a grade and 3 rewrites. Tips he gives: "the more competitors you add in one analysis, the muddier it gets", so run one competitor per pass; your own channel's data is the best source.

**F12. Delivery checklist before recording a hook (ZM3):** shake out your body for 30 seconds → throwaway reps → friend photo by the lens → line by line → "this does not matter" reset → short sentences → breathers on the key word → downward inflection → no dropping phrases → believe the words (rework AI scripts for 10 to 20 minutes) → happy to be there.

## 3. Applying it to a business coach's talking-head reel

- **Line one = subject + outcome, framed with "you".** Swap "I spent years figuring out sales calls" for "If your sales calls end in 'let me think about it', it's this one line" (Delay/Irrelevance fix, P3).
- **One standard deviation away (P16):** do not hook "make more money" or "get more clients" head-on. Hook the tactical pain that leads there: "your DMs", "your discovery call opener", "your offer page", "your follow-up". The buyer makes the leap to revenue.
- **Contrarian snapback (P4):** "Most coaches think they need more leads. But the problem is almost never lead volume. It's what happens in the first 5 minutes of the call." (The structure is his; the claim has to be the client's real belief and real answer.)
- **Text hook (P8):** subject repeater + pain, max 2 lines, no connectors: `DISCOVERY CALLS / THAT GO NOWHERE` or `YOUR OFFER PAGE / IS LOSING BUYERS`. Use the client's real numbers when they have them ("$40K MONTH FROM 3 CALLS A WEEK" only if it is true).
- **Lock-in (P15):** coaches usually have client results, so default to **tease the proof** ("One client changed this one line and booked 9 calls in a week", only with a real case), then **magic box** (name the client's method: "I call it the 2-question close"). If there is no proof, use **transformation** ("…and calls stop feeling like begging"). If the topic is old ("how to handle objections"), use **rapid contrast** or **objection handling** ("and no, this isn't another script you have to memorize").
- **Visual hook (P10):** a talking head needs only a small move: lean in, a punch-in on the first word, the text sliding in, a hand gesture showing the gap from "where you are" to "where you want to be" (P17). Don't over-edit; text + spoken carry it.
- **Proof flashes (P22):** right after the hook, cut a 1-second screenshot (a client DM, Stripe, calendar of booked calls) over the line. Only real artifacts.
- **Caption hook (P6):** the caption's first line should restate the hook in plain words, not "🔥 Big one today".
- **Fear (P18):** coaches can show the fear coming to life without saying it outright: "This is what an empty calendar in month 3 looks like" over a screen of an empty calendar, then tease the fix early.
- **Recording direction (P21):** have the client record the first two lines last or after throwaway takes, line by line, with falling inflection and a breather after the key word. If Sam wrote the script, have the coach rework it into their own words for 10 to 20 minutes before recording.
- **Power words (P9):** mine the client's own top 10 reels and their competitors' for repeated phrases ("client-getting machine", "booked out") and twist them; never copy a hook format from an unrelated niche.

## 4. Diagnosing a prospect's reel in a Loom/mockup (what to look for)

Run in this order and stop at the first big failure (the skill's order, plus Kallaway's new checks):
1. **Delay:** count seconds or lines until the topic is named. If it isn't in the first sentence, give the timestamp ("the topic shows up at 0:04, after two lines of warm-up"). Watch for throat-clearing openers: "So…", "Okay guys", "I want to talk about…", "Let me tell you a story". With a roughly 4-second timer (P13), anything after it might as well not exist for most viewers.
2. **Confusion:** read the hook alone. Is there more than one reading? Passive voice, long clauses, jargon? Rewrite it at a sixth-grade level as the mockup fix.
3. **Irrelevance:** count I/me vs you/your in the first 2 sentences. "I" openers and nice-to-have framing ("3 trends in coaching") are a flag. Is it a need-to-have pain?
4. **Disinterest:** is there any contrast (a belief vs an alternative, stated or implied)? If the hook just states a fact the viewer already believes, there is no loop.
5. **Hedged question vs declarative:** "Do you struggle with…?" vs a flat claim. Kallaway's data: declaratives win.
6. **Triple alignment:** mute the video. Does the text hook say the same thing as the spoken line? Does the visual show it? Is there a text hook at all (vs captions only)? Is it over 2 lines, full of connectors, covering the face, or gone before about 3 seconds?
7. **Text hook build:** is there a subject repeater (the actual term) + pain/outcome/state change? Vague numbers where a specific one exists?
8. **Visual:** any motion in the first second, or a static locked-off face? (Fix: a small lean, a punch-in, text animating in.)
9. **Lock-in zone (seconds 3 to 10):** do the next 1 to 3 sentences confirm the hook and give a reason to trust (proof, a named method, an objection handled), or do they jump to the body or wander into backstory? Name which of the six methods would fit. This is often the best Loom point because few people talk about it.
10. **Delivery:** upspeak on the hook line, energy dropping at sentence ends, rushed key words with no pause, reading AI-sounding lines they clearly don't own.
11. **Caption first line:** does it restate the hook, or waste the space above the fold?
12. **One standard deviation check:** if the hook pitches "make more money" or "6 figures" head-on, flag the BS-detector risk and suggest the tactical-pain version.
Loom framing: one problem, a timestamp, the business effect ("the right coach scrolls before hearing what you fix"), one on-screen fix (a rewritten text hook + spoken line in the mockup).

## 5. Contradictions or things that changed (older → newer)

- **C1. Visual vs text importance.** Old (LmXp): "visual hooks are probably 100 times more powerful than just spoken word hooks… people read faster than they can hear", and "visual hooks" there includes the title text. New (pNIY, 2 months ago): text hook is "the most important hook component by far"; the visual hook is "the least important": yappers win with text + spoken only. The skill's order "TEXT, then VISUAL, then SPOKEN" matches neither exactly. The newest is effectively TEXT > SPOKEN > VISUAL for talking heads. Suggest updating the skill, or flag it UNRESOLVED for Sam.
- **C2. Text hook length.** Old: "three to five words… big bold font". New: up to 2 lines of subject repeater + one modifier, and premium typography can carry the full spoken line word for word (3 lines with one dominant). Not a hard conflict, but the 3-to-5-word rule is gone.
- **C3. How long the hook is and where the lock-in starts.** 2byPP: hooks are "really two to three lines". LmXp: the formula fits "three or four sentences". pNIY: hook = "first three to five seconds", lock-in = "next five to 10 seconds… two to four sentences". vmsD (newest): hook = "the first sentence you say and the two to three seconds you show on screen", lock-in = "one to three sentences". g_6 (old, long-form): the first three checkpoints take "10 to 30 seconds"; his own took 24 s / 12 sentences. The trend over time: the hook got shorter (one sentence) and the contrast/proof work moved into a named lock-in zone. The skill's "lock-in zone seconds 3 to 15" is close enough; the newest framing is one line of hook + 1 to 3 lock-in sentences.
- **C4. Where the contrast goes.** LmXp puts the whole contrast inside the hook (lean, but, snapback). vmsD moves "rapid contrast" into the lock-in zone and uses it only when the premise is dull. Both work; the newer advice treats contrast as one option among six for line 2.
- **C5. Energy amount.** g_6 (old): "bring your energy of delivery up by like 50%". The skill says 10 to 20% (from another source). ZM3 gives no number. UNRESOLVED; don't quote either as fact.
- **C6. "I" openers.** 2byPP and g_6: never open with I/me ("you" 9 times, "I" 0). ZM3: admission lines ("I hate recording") build likability, and the Brick/Light Phone hooks open with "Ever feel like your phone controls…" (you), then "it changed everything for me". Ruling: the opener is "you"; "I" stories and admissions go in the lock-in or body. That matches the skill's rule.
- **C7. Copying hooks.** LmXp: don't use lists of proven hooks, learn the psychology. pNIY: format remixing is fine but only "level 1". a7Vj: cross-niche copy-paste fails, while same-lane, data-backed remix with a grading rubric works. This is consistent, and it sharpened over time.
- **C8. Proof placement.** The older videos treat proof as checkpoint 2 (trust, after the pain is accepted). The newer ones (pNIY, vmsD) put proof right after the hook as the main lock-in lever. Same idea, but the newer advice moves it earlier.
- **C9. Cult hopping / metaphors (LmXp) vs Sam's voice rule** (no invented metaphors or celebrity scenarios). That is a conflict with Sam's skill, not inside Kallaway. Only use real, known references.
- **C10. Pain-first vs curiosity-first.** Iyw/g_6 (old) say hammer the pain first; short form should be more explicit about fear. pNIY (new) frames stakes as the wanted outcome ("more customers, more money") and says entertainment stakes are just curiosity. Both fit the "pain point reminder OR desired outcome teaser" choice in the text formula.

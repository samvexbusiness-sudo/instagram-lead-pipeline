# Editor niche on Instagram: what gets views (30 Oct 2026)

Data: 54 video-editor accounts under 100k followers, 505 reels (Apify profile + search scrapes), plus samvexedits. Script: `research/editor_niche.py`. Format is read from captions (297 reels unclassifiable), covers checked visually for the top 8 and bottom 8 small accounts. Correlation, not proof.

## Numbers
| Follower band | Median views per reel |
|---|---|
| <500 | 67 |
| 500-2k | 487 |
| 2k-10k | ~1,000 |
| samvexedits (7) | 17 (beats 7 of 41 small accounts) |

Within an account, format barely moves views (lift vs the account's own median): tutorial 1.05, before/after 1.04, showreel 1.00, tool news 0.97, story 0.95.
Reels that tag/mention the creator in the footage: lift 1.08, 28% beat 2x the account median (vs 19% without).
CTA: comment-keyword 1.00, DM 0.91, project file 0.80 (11 reels only).
Posting frequency: top small accounts post 0.2 to 2.5 a week; the 21-35/week accounts are near the bottom.

## What the top small accounts (<2k followers, 400 to 1,700 median views) show on screen
1. Real client work for real businesses, client tagged (ads.reels.editor: 122 followers, 5 posts, 1,391 median; local food shop ads. dukeeditor: local business ads).
2. The editor's face and story (ash_editorbali "Day 2 as a video editor living in Bali" 3.1k, "took the biggest risk of my life"; thegoodnesseni "i edited this 5 minute VSL for an AI creator in 16 hours").
3. Before/after with a big readable hook word and a price contrast in a clean template ($5 vs $80: dukeeditor, bigeditor.60fps; halal_editor "Do you want thousands").
Bottom accounts: "Send me DM" graphic slides, before/afters with tiny labels and no hook text, testimonials, random off-niche posts.

## Decision
- Main format: before/after of real spec/client edits (unique footage, creator tagged), Duke-style template with a big hook word + price contrast.
- Second format, ~1 a week: the editor's own face/process story ("POV: I edited this coach's reel in X hours").
- Drop: re-edits of shared practice footage, "DM me" graphics, tutorials (views fine, wrong audience).

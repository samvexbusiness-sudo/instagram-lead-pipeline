## 2. The pipeline (built, working)

**Commands:**
- `python run.py status`
- `discover`
- `snowball` (lookalikes from the best leads, with a free name screen first)
- `prefilter`
- `qualify` (manual batch through chat, or the API)
- `import-verdicts`
- `contacts`
- `export`

**Safety:**
- **Budget caps on every paid call:** Apify $2 a run, $1.50 a day, $35 a month. Claude API $3 a day, $75 a month. Hunter 45 credits a month.
- **Spend needs `--approve-spend`.** Every spend is written to `data/spend_ledger.jsonl`.

  - at least 30% video, at least 40% original audio

## 3. Cold email: what we found

**My sent mail (about 200 threads):** about 6 human replies, 3 positive (Lia, Char, Vivian).
- The copy itself is fine. The leaks were:
  - Wrong targets: social media managers, agencies, ops people, accountants.
  - support@ helpdesk inboxes.
  - A pitch about "the opening" instead of the coach's money and leads.
- The Loom variant did worse.

**4 YouTube cold-email videos, what to keep:**
- The value is the problem you solve (their leads).
- Plain, short emails.
- A 3rd touch around day 7.
- Reply fast.
- IG or portfolio link in the signature.
- Re-email non-repliers after 3-6 months.

What to skip from them: webinars, high volume, warmed domains, and the "don't personalize" advice.

**Deliverability tests (30 Oct):**
- **mail-tester.com: 10/10.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC pass, not on any blocklist, and the image attachment cost 0 points.
- **Outlook seed test: lands in Junk.**
  - Same result with the image, without the image, and from an Outlook account not linked to my Gmail.
  - Outlook also blocks the attachment, so the mockup is invisible to Outlook users.
  - Cause: cold email from a free @gmail.com address.
- **Where past recipients' email is hosted** (110 addresses): **85% Google, 5% Microsoft, 5% small hosts, 3% Yahoo/AOL/iCloud, 2% dead** (2 wasted sends).
- **Decision: keep sending from Gmail.** The Junk problem only hits about 5% of leads. Revisit a custom domain (about $7/mo Google Workspace + about $12/yr domain, with a 2-3 week warm-up) only if I go past about 30 a day or target Microsoft-heavy niches.

**Proposed A/B test (not started):**
- **A:** current email with the mockup attached, plus "want the full edit?"
- **B:** no attachment, subject "your {topic} reel, {Name}", ending "I mocked up a new opening… want me to send it over?"
- About 20-30 of each. The variant goes in a Notion column and a Gmail label.

## 5. My Instagram (samvexedits): audit and niche data

**My account:** 7 followers, 22 posts. The last 12 reels got 3-51 views (median 17) and 0 comments.
- The recent ones already used Duke's template: a small raw card next to the big edited card, price tags, and a "Comment X" bar.
- Several of them also had the "Comment PROJECT for the project file" CTA. So the CTA wasn't the cause.
- The same caption was pasted on 3 posts. The footage was the same viral practice footage every editor uses.

**Niche data** (54 editor accounts under 100k followers, 505 reels; script `research/editor_niche.py`):

| Followers | Median views per reel |
|---|---|
| Under 500 | 67 |
| 500-2k | 487 |
| 2k-10k | about 1,000 |
| Me (7) | 17 (better than only 7 of 41 small accounts) |

- **Format barely changes views within the same account.** Compared with each account's own median: tutorial 1.05x, before/after 1.04x, showreel 1.00x, tool news 0.97x, story 0.95x. The account level is what moves views.
- **Tagging the creator helps:** 1.08x, and 28% of tagged reels beat twice the account's median vs 19% without.
- **CTAs:** "comment KEYWORD" 1.00x, "DM me" 0.91x.
- **Posting more doesn't help:** the top small accounts post 0.2-2.5 times a week. The accounts posting 21-35 a week are near the bottom.

**What the top small accounts show on screen:**
1. **Real client work for real businesses, with the client tagged.** ads.reels.editor has 122 followers and 5 posts and gets a 1,391 median on local food-shop ads. Duke does local business ads.
2. **The editor's own face and story.** "Day 2 as a video editor living in Bali" got 3.1k. "I edited this 5-minute VSL for an AI creator in 16 hours" got 1.2k.
3. **A before/after with a big hook word and a $5-vs-$80 price contrast** in a clean template (Duke, bigeditor, halal_editor).

The bottom accounts post "Send me DM" graphics, before/afters with tiny labels and no hook, and testimonials.

**Decision:**
- **Main format:** before/afters of real spec and client edits (unique footage, creator tagged) in the Duke-style template, with a big hook word that names the viewer plus the price contrast.
- **About 1 a week:** my own face-and-process story ("POV: I edited this coach's reel in X hours").
- **Stop:** re-edits of shared practice footage, "DM me" graphics, tutorials (fine views, wrong audience).
- **Judge after 10 posts.** Use Trial Reels (shown only to non-followers) while followers are under ~500.
- Caveats: format was read from captions (297 reels couldn't be classified), covers were only checked visually for 16 accounts, and this is correlation, not proof.

## 6. Kallaway research (43 videos)

Files: `knowledge/kallaway/PLAYBOOK.md` (start here), `HANDOFF.md`, `notes/*.md` (hooks, story, growth, brand, AI), `SOURCES.md`.

Top takeaways:
1. **The idea is the bottleneck.** Each reel covers one buyer pain the paid offer solves. Off-offer topics grow followers who won't buy.
2. **Every reel needs a ramp:** keyword → freebie on the same topic → offer. Sales = signal × trust × ramp × offer, so any zero means zero sales.
3. **Hook:** topic and stakes in the first sentence, a flat "you" statement. Hook the tactical pain (the discovery call), not "make more money".
4. **The text hook matters most for talking heads** (text > spoken > visual). It must match the spoken line. The caption's first line is a 4th hook.
5. **The 1-3 sentences after the hook decide retention.** For coaches: tease the proof, then name the method.
6. **Weekly mix "3-2-1":** 3 broad-but-in-niche, 2 narrow, 1 experimental.
   - Research outliers (5x the account's average) on accounts with 10k-250k followers.
   - Judge by leads per view, in batches of 10.
7. **The editor's 2026 lever:** a signature visual style per client, because AI edits are all converging on one look.
8. **Open decisions where my skill and Kallaway disagree:**
   - How much to change when copying a winner.
   - How many repeats after a 10x reel.
   - How much extra on-camera energy.
   - Whether text should come first in the hook order.

The playbook's section 6 has a paste-ready block for my `sam-content-brain` skill.
